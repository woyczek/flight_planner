# Company — manual

The Company app is the airline pilot's: the missions the airline offers (Missions module). It is where you choose your next
flight, or draw it among those you have not flown yet; « Prepare this flight » then goes to the Pilot app, in Fuel, with
the mission prepared. The flight in progress is the same in both apps.

## First start

- **The entries.** Missions, and Fleet (the current airline's, at the Full level), in the bottom bar of a phone and in the left column of a screen. Top right,
  « ? » opens this help and ⚙ the Settings.
- **The apps.** The application's name, top left, opens the list of applications (your airline's name under « Company »):
  one tap goes to Pilot.
- **The help.** It opens at the chapter of the screen shown; « All help », at the top, leads to the other manuals and the
  tutorials.
- **The logo.** The app's icon is your airline's logo when the data repository has one (a png or webp file of 512 KB at
  most, named in airline/settings.json: "logo": "airline/logo.png"); otherwise, the tinted plane.

# Missions

The airline's missions, in the Company application: those of the shared repository (written with the CPCO): choose one, or draw one at random, then prepare it in Fuel.

## How to use

<a id="missions-assigned"></a>**My assigned missions.** When the airline's centre assigns you a mission, it appears at the head of Missions, under "My assigned missions": the take-off (aerodrome, time Z), the aircraft, the callsign, and the crew's rendezvous and channels when the assignment carries them ("Open" for a link, "Copy" for a server or a frequency); "Prepare this flight" opens it in Fuel. The group exists only when an assignment carries your trigram; a cancelled assignment, or one whose take-off is more than 12 h past, is no longer there. Assignments are readable by every pilot of the airline: keep session passwords for the channel itself.

<a id="missions-list"></a>**The list.** The missions of the newest series first, grouped by family. Only the missions whose file is on this device are offered, in the list as in the draw; when there is none, the screen says "No readable mission on this device: synchronise". Each row gives the id, the title, the route, the aircraft type and the estimated block; "flown on …" marks a mission whose report you wrote. The search filters on the id, the title, an aerodrome (ICAO code), an aircraft type, a callsign (SAMU66) or the activity (MEDEVAC, HEMS, SAMU…), and also in the content of the missions already on the device: summary, description, objectives, risks, places, notes, callsigns and types, ignoring case, accents and hyphens ("clermont-ferrand" = "Clermont Ferrand"). A place that only a drawer cites is not searched, even once the drawer is open: the search never gives a closed drawer away. When the word is not in the title, the row says where it was found ("in: risks"); a mission whose file is not on the device yet is searched on its registry line, and the list says so ("n missions not read"). Nothing is written or downloaded to search. The registry search is the same; the drop-down list keeps one family, given by its letter (the one of the identifiers), its short name and its name ("T · AIRLIFT · Airlift / logistics"), in letter order; a chosen family with several subtypes opens a second one, "Subtype", beside it. Seven rows are shown, "N more" unfolds the rest. The organisation missions of an exercise (those ending in Z) are not flights and are not listed. Only the missions of the airline chosen in Settings › Airline are listed; a mission that names no airline is personal: it is under « Personnel ». The list « My airline · Personal · All airlines », above the search, also shows the personal missions or those of other airlines, each with the name of its airline; they can be prepared the same way. When the airline has categories (cargo, passengers…), a second drop-down keeps one category, and the open mission says its own.

<a id="missions-open"></a>**Choose, or draw at random.** A tap on a row opens the mission sheet, its Brief: its category, its line of choice (duration · aircraft · theatre, as in the registry), its summary in full under the title "Summary" (beyond 600 signs, "Read more" opens the detail), its risks (the first three, "hazard → mitigation", the others folded, or "No risk declared"), the teams engaged and its zones. "Detail" shows the rest in foldable sections: description (the detailed description only, without the summary), objectives, zones to activate, rendezvous, route provided, maps provided, payload and aircraft; "See on the Map" opens the Map on a zone or on a map of the mission (drawn from its coordinates). On a phone, the browser's back returns from the detail to the sheet, then to the list; on a large screen, the sheet and the detail are side by side. "Draw at random" picks one among the missions of the filtered list you have not flown yet; "Draw again" picks another. Nothing starts by itself: "Prepare this flight" opens Fuel, in the Pilot application, with the departure, the arrival, the points of the mission (as coordinates), the level and the aircraft type; the runway, the procedures, the fuel and the weather are then prepared as for any flight.

<a id="missions-drawers"></a>**The rest of the mission.** A mission may have a sequel that nothing announces: it is given to you in flight. On the sheet of YOUR mission — it is assigned to you, or it is the mission of your flight — it then appears under “Rest of the mission”: its title, how and when (“opened on entering the zone, at 20:10Z”), its message and its briefing; a notification says so. Nothing appears before. “Ask for what comes next”, next to “Prepare this flight”, is touched when you have reached or found what the mission asks for: the centre gives you what comes next if there is anything, otherwise it answers “Nothing new for now”; the command then rests for a minute. During the mission's flight, with a live position (this computer's simulator, the GPS, your online session), your device follows the flight without saying anything; a landing asked for by the mission also counts when your device knows you are on the ground (simulator, IVAO), and so does an overflight, or a hover held within a band of heights above the ground, when it knows it (simulator only), and a retardant refill — down at the station, on the heading asked, for the time asked, then gone again (simulator only); a sequence of steps opens when all are done, in order; without a live position, what comes next arrives at its time. A precision landing on a point is judged in tiers — excellent, perfect, good, partial — which the line of the sequel states with the distance to the target point; for an arrival at an airfield and for a required stopover, the margin is fixed, no longer the radius written in the mission file. What is given to a crew mate on their finding is given to you too — within a few seconds while your sheet is on screen, and the line says “received here at …” when it came later. At the Full level, “Reseal” closes again a part you opened, for you only. As long as a part is not given, the sheet and its Detail neither list nor name what it hides; a mission you are not playing shows only its visible part. The device keeps to itself what was given to you (nothing is sent): the Map uses it. When a part has opened on a precision landing, a block “Debrief” appears under the sequel: one line per landing judged, yours and your crew mates', with the tier and the distance to the target point, computed from your own mission file.

<a id="missions-assign"></a>**Assigning a mission.** At the Everyday level, “Assign…” in the sheet of a mission opens “Assign this mission”: the pilot (a trigram of the airline, or three letters), the callsign, the take-off in UTC and the aircraft — a type of the mission and, if you wish, a registration; the departure aerodrome is the mission's. If the pilot already has a mission less than two hours away, the screen says so and you can assign anyway. The assignments of the mission are listed at the top; “Remove” cancels one. The pilot finds the mission in “My assigned missions”. No one checks rights or availability: they remain with your airline's organisation.

<a id="missions-add"></a>**Add missions.** « Add missions », next to « Draw at random », reads one or more YAML mission files (a file may hold several, separated by « --- » or in a `missions:` list), or a pasted YAML — or an assistant’s whole answer: its code blocks (« ```yaml … ``` », else « ``` … ``` ») are read, each as a source, and the text around them is ignored (the screen says so); an indentation with tabs is refused (YAML is indented with spaces) and an error is given with the line of the pasted text. Before writing, the screen shows what it read (id, title, family, series, airline) and what it refuses, with the reason (id outside the rule, exercise organisation, duplicate, YAML unreadable at a given line). « Add (n) » writes each mission at its place, `missions/<letter>_<FAMILY>/<ID>/mission<ID>.yaml`; a mission that already exists is replaced only when « replace? » is ticked. A mission without an airline is personal. They leave at the next synchronisation. The missions added are entered in the registry at once: they open in the workshop without « Update the registry ».

<a id="missions-airline"></a>**Change airline.** On a mission’s sheet, « Airline: … · Change » offers the airlines and « Personal »; the sentence « … moves from … to … » is said before « Write ». In the list, « Assign to an airline… » takes the missions shown (scope, family, search), each ticked: the screen says how many change and which are unreadable (left as they are) before writing. When adding from YAML, « Add these missions to » chooses their airline. Only the `airline` field of the file changes.

<a id="missions-registry"></a>**The registry.** « Registry », next to « Draw at random » (or the address `#missions?view=registry`), shows one line per mission as the registry `missions/mission_registry.yaml` writes it, with the state of its file: there, missing, unreadable or path refused; and the repository missions the registry does not know. On a computer the registry is a table: the state of the mission (not in the registry, to correct, read, not read — the screen reads 500 files at most and says so in the count), the identifier, the title, the family, the series, the origin and the destination, the duration, then « ⋯ »; each column title sorts (a second tap reverses); on a phone, the same list, one mission per card, and a single « Sort » button that opens the choice of the column and the direction. The default order is the identifier, the most recent on top; the sort chosen is kept on this device, and « Reset the order » goes back to the default. Touching a line opens its sheet, on the right of the table (full screen on a phone, « ← Registry » or the browser's Back returns to the list): the state in words and the file, the route, the family, the series, the number, the operation, the duration, the aircraft and the theatre, then « Brief » (the mission sheet as the pilot sees it, never the content of a closed drawer; « Back to the registry » returns to the same sheet), « Workshop », « Duplicate » and « ⋯ » (« Correct the registry line », « Remove from the registry »). « ⋯ » at the end of a line gives the same gestures without leaving the table. A repository mission the registry does not know (added before the import entered it) has, in its sheet and its « ⋯ », « Enter in the registry »: its line is written from its file alone; opening the registry never writes anything. At the head of the registry, each draft of the workshop kept on this device has its line: « Resume » reopens the workshop as you left it, « Discard » erases it after a confirmation. The line of choice says what the mission file says; a missing duration is computed on the route given, at the cruise speed of the file or at a typical speed of the aircraft's class (helicopter, aeroplane), rounded to five minutes and marked « ≈ »; the theatre is the file's, else the State of the departure aerodrome; what is not known is not shown. The family filter (and its subtype, as in the list) and the search are at the head. « Update the registry » first says what it would do (missions to add, lines without a readable file, duplicate identifiers, former identifiers), then « Write the registry » adds the repository missions, keeps one line per identifier and replaces a former identifier by the folder's; a line without a readable file stays (the file may not have arrived yet). An added mission takes its file's series, else the current series of its family (a number). In an identifier (« A-001A »), the final letter from A to Y is a flight linked to the mission without suffix, Z its organisation. « Correct the registry line » changes the title, series, number or operation of a line; « Remove from the registry » takes it out, the mission file stays in the repository. An unreadable registry is never rewritten; an absent one is created from the files. Nothing, in the registry or the Brief, says that a mission goes beyond its first objective; the box « See the full content of the missions », unticked each time Missions opens and never saved, makes the workshop open the whole mission. It is a secret of the screen: the mission file stays readable by whoever opens it.

<a id="missions-editor"></a>**The editor.** In the registry, « Create a mission » (at the head), « Workshop » and « Duplicate » (in the sheet of a line and its « ⋯ »; « Workshop » also in the Brief opened from the registry) open the editor, which has the shape of a workshop. A mission that exists opens veiled — you too may discover it by playing it: the workshop shows only what the pilot sees at the departure (« At the departure », its places, its route), saying nothing of what might follow; « Reveal the whole mission », in the unfolding, shows everything after a question (« Reveal » or « Keep the secret ») — this button is on every veiled mission, whether it hides something or not. Veiled, the mission is retouched (texts, positions, places added) and saved without losing anything hidden; removing an existing place or changing its identifier asks to reveal it. A mission you create is whole, a copy stays veiled. The workshop: the map of the mission (the suite's map, where the mission is drawn; touching a place chooses its row in the Places, « Recenter » brings the view back; offline, the map shows the places on a plain background and everything else works; under the map, « Background » chooses « Fast » or « Relief » (the topographic background) and « Airspaces » shows the R, D and P zones and the RTBA of the view, under the mission — this choice belongs to the mission: it is saved with it, and the pilot's map opens so; with no map at all, a sketch of the places), the unfolding, a list from top to bottom (« At the departure », then one drawer per row — its mark, its rank, its title and its key —, and « ＋ Drawer · Choose a brick », which opens the window « Choose a drawer brick » (full screen on a phone): a field « Search for a brick… », a line of counts, then groups to unfold — « Cross-cutting actions » first (precision landing, hover, back to one's aerodrome), one group per theme and, in it, a family and its bricks (each its symbol, its title and its key; its brief on hover), last « A key alone » (the pilot's gesture alone, an hour, a passage zone, an object to find, the arrival at the aerodrome…, without text); one touch creates the drawer with its key, its title and its message, closes the window, opens its sheet and says so in a notification whose « Undo » removes it; « Close » or Escape closes the window without creating anything; only the bricks whose key the flight can judge are offered, and the places they need stay to be placed — touching a row opens its sheet in place of the departure's: what opens the drawer (« Change the key » opens the same window: a key alone changes the key, a brick gives its key and its settings without touching your texts; a passage zone or an object to find are chosen among the Places; the pilot's gesture alone: he opens it by « Ask for what comes next »), its fallback time when the key looks at the aircraft (without a position received from the aircraft, the drawer opens at that time), « Show this zone to the pilot from the departure » (unticked by default: the passage zone stays hidden until the opening), its title — which the pilot reads once the drawer is open —, its message, its briefing, its onward route place by place, « What the pilot sees » — nothing while the drawer is closed (the pilot does not know it exists), then the open drawer —, and « Remove this drawer »; an arrival can require stops, added and removed one by one; « Try the keys », in the head of the unfolding, opens the trial in place of the sheet: the aircraft of the trial follows the route of the departure, you move the time on a slider, and the log says what opens each drawer (« zone entered », « object found », « landed at the arrival », the hour, the fallback time) or why it stays closed (« above its ceiling », « unknown — the altitude is missing »); the map shows the aircraft and its trail, each row says « open at H + 6 min » or « closed »; « Place the aircraft here », beside the line under the tools of the map, places the aircraft of the trial where you then touch the map, at the minute of the slider — a zone you place it in opens, an object within range is found; placing is not flying: a zone between the former position and the new one is not crossed; when it is within range of the object or in the zone of a drawer while an earlier drawer is closed, the log says so (« nothing opens, drawer 1 first »); « Start again » forgets the placements; it is the very computation of the flight, and the trial writes nothing in the mission; twelve drawers at most; a key the workshop cannot edit yet is kept as it is) and the sheet of the departure, what the pilot reads before leaving, in five groups (Frame, Teams, Story, Risks, Timeline and maps), under the frame « Places » at the head of the right column (on a phone, just under the map): its list of places, of fixed height, scrolls in itself; touching a row chooses the place, on the map too, and opens its fields under the list; touching a place on the map brings its row into view in the list. Each group says its content in one line; touching it unfolds it, touching it again folds it, one is unfolded at a time. On a phone the map unfolds by its button and a mission being edited opens with its groups folded; on a large screen the map and the unfolding are on the left, the sheet on the right. Under the map, « Profile » shows the terrain under the route of the part shown (the Map's vertical profile) and is redrawn when a place is moved; folded on a phone, open on a large screen; without terrain (offline, a refused tile) it says « Terrain unavailable » and draws the route without it, and with fewer than two places it asks you to place two; each place of the route is named under the axis, and when the terrain is read and a cruise level is written, a line says it when the cruise goes below the terrain (« The cruise goes below the terrain at 3.5 NM (625 ft). ») or keeps less than 500 ft of margin. At the foot, « Save » and « Cancel » side by side — « Cancel » leaves the workshop; if the mission carries an unsaved change, the workshop asks first: « Leave » or « Stay » (the words of the frame, which says the draft keeps everything: « Leave? Your changes stay as a draft on this device. »; the same when you leave the workshop by the menu, by the « Missions » tab or by the browser's « Back »: the workshop tells the frame, which asks and keeps it intact if you stay) — then « To complete (n) », which opens, just above, the named list of what is missing, never blocking. The map shows: departure and arrival in blue (for a liaison, their position comes from the aeronautical database), the target in red, the zones by their circle or their outline, the turning points, the rendezvous, the objects to find, and the route of the departure as a dashed line. Each place carries its identifier on it. Under the map, « View of the map » chooses what it shows: « Everything — the author's view », or the pilot's view, at the departure then after each drawer — the map then draws only what the pilot may see at that stage (a place cited by his route, by one of his maps or by a drawer already open; the zone of a closed drawer when you show it), and the tools come back with « Everything ». In a mission with drawers, a place, a zone or an object that nothing cites — neither the route, nor a map, nor a drawer — will never be shown to the pilot: « To complete » names it. The route of the departure is read as chips under the list of the Places: a cross removes a place, « Add a place… » adds one, and a place touched is chosen: the two arrows « ← Earlier » and « Later → » move it by one rank (in the onward route of a drawer too); in the route of the departure, the aerodrome of departure stays first and the aerodrome of arrival last — they are neither moved nor removed, and a place added enters before the arrival; on the map, the « Route » tool adds each place touched to the route of the departure — or, when a drawer is chosen in the unfolding, to its onward route — and stays in hand until it is taken again (« Undo the gesture » gives back the last place added this way). The map also shows, as small dots, the landmarks of the aeronautical database (aerodromes, beacons, fixes) when one gets closer: touching one says what it is, creating nothing, and « Add as a point » makes of it a turning point of the mission. Under the map, « Find a landmark » finds it by its code or its name: the map centres on it and circles it, as if you had touched it; when several landmarks answer, they are offered under the field. Above the map, the other tools: « Selection » (touching a place chooses it — it says its name, its line opens —, dragging it moves it; a round zone chosen shows the handle of its radius; the departure and the arrival are not moved), « Point », « Zone » and « Object » (a touch on the map poses the place there, then the tool goes back to « Selection »: nothing is created by accident); « Undo the gesture » gives back the places of before the last gesture on the map, never a typing, and « Redo » plays the last undone gesture again (any new gesture empties it). « Zone » poses the shape the tool shows, a circle or a polygon (an outline of six vertices): a second touch on the tool already chosen, or a long press, changes it (« Change to polygon », « Change to circle »). Every button of the map says its name on hover and to a screen reader. These buttons are icons of the suite (Selection, Point, Route, Zone — a circle or a polygon according to its shape —, Object, Undo the gesture, Redo, Recenter, and the Fast and Relief backgrounds); the tool in hand stays marked. A round zone becomes an outline by « Change to polygon », in its sheet: six vertices at its radius; chosen, it shows its vertices (they are dragged), between two vertices a point that adds one, and « Remove this vertex » for the vertex touched (three at least); its middle moves it as a block; the map frames it when its outline is drawn or when it is chosen (« Recenter » comes back to the mission); « Change to circle » gives its circle back. From the sheet of a drawer, « Draw on the map » (or « Pose on the map » for an object) poses the zone that becomes its key. The map background is asked of its provider (tiles.openfreemap.org), as for the Map: it receives the area shown, hence that of the mission; offline, nothing is asked. The Frame group (family and subtype, series, identifier, title, theatre, departure, arrival) and the Teams group (one row per team: callsign, type, service, role; « + Team » adds one, the cross removes it) and the Places group (one row per place: departure and arrival for their position, target, zones, turning points, rendezvous, with their position in degrees and minutes; touching it opens its fields, under the list, latitude and longitude in decimal degrees; « + Zone », « + Turning point », « + Rendezvous », « + Target », « + Object to find » (an object the pilot does not see while the drawer whose key it is stays closed: its kind, its position, its detection range, « found only when the pilot declares it »; its identifier is given by the workshop; its row, like the row of a zone, says the drawer that cites it: « key of drawer 2 »); a polygon keeps its points, still moved in the V2; renaming a place renames it wherever the mission cites it (the route, a drawer, a map, the timeline, the profile); « Remove » on a place still cited first says who cites it, then « Remove anyway » removes it with its citations, and « To complete » then says the drawer left without its key (its trigger point, its passage zone, the point of its arrival) or the map left without its centre) and the Story group (the summary, with its count of signs: the sheet shows it whole up to 600; the description, one paragraph per blank line; the main objective; the secondary objectives, one per line; the load, its mass and its passengers) and the Risks group (one row « hazard → mitigation » per risk — the sheet of the mission shows three on its first page; « Edit » opens the hazard, the consequence and the mitigation; « + Risk » adds one; a removed risk leaves its translations too) are filled there; what is typed is kept from group to group until « Save » or « Cancel ». The last one, Timeline and maps, gives the mission's events (one row per event: the time from the planned take-off, « H+30mn », a place of the mission or none, what happens; « + Event ») then the declared maps; « Propose maps from the places » adds the missing ones (general, target, rendezvous, zones), whose images are made in the V2 map studio. The organisation of an exercise (Z mission) stays in the V2. A commercial mission (family « E · CAT », a link flight) has a reduced Frame: departure, arrival, airline, load (passengers, cargo…) and aircraft; the title is proposed by itself (« LFPO → LFRS ») as long as you have not changed it; teams, risks, timeline and theatre are not asked. The family proposes the next identifier and the current series; only a missing family and an identifier without its form (« T-086 ») or already taken prevent saving, the rest is listed under « To complete ». « Save » writes the mission file and its registry line on the device: they go with the next send, and the editor says so (« changed on this device — not sent yet »). Only what you change is written, the rest of the file is kept as it is; « Save » without any change writes nothing. A file that uses YAML aliases (« &… », « *… ») does not open in the editor, and a registry that uses them is said unreadable and never rewritten: write the repeated object out in full at each place (or edit the mission in the V2); a changed identifier writes a new file, the former one is never deleted. A mission created without an airline is personal. Departure and Arrival are the aerodrome field of the suite, the one of Fuel: two letters propose the aerodromes of the base (seven at most, chosen with the keyboard or by touch), the field carries the code and the name is read under it; a place that is not an aerodrome is written as typed. A helistation (two letters and three digits) is chosen the same way. A turning point is also chosen among the points of the base (beacons, reporting points, network points): the Name field of its sheet (like Departure and Arrival) proposes them by their code and, once one is chosen, the place takes its position; free text stays as typed; with a landmark of the base chosen on the map, « + Turning point » and « + Target » add that landmark; a place name typed that is a code of the base gets its position. A named point of the base (aerodrome, helistation, beacon, reporting point), chosen or typed — in the departure and the arrival of the Cadre too —, writes its complete position in the place and locks it: the latitude and the longitude are read only, the sheet says « Position from the aeronautical base: the aerodrome LFPO — locked », and the map does not move it; the route of the departure and the profile go through it. To free it, erase its code: the place keeps its position, as a free point you can move. A known code typed or chosen on a place already placed moves it to the position from the base; a notification says so, and its « Undo » gives back its former position and name (a free name moves nothing, nor does a typed code that the base gives to several points: choose the right one in the list). In the Teams, a team's callsign completes as everywhere else: from two characters the callsigns of the known fleet are proposed with their type; choosing one writes the callsign and fills the line's ICAO type when only one type is known. A free text stays as typed, and nothing is written until you type or choose. The bricks of the window of « ＋ Drawer » are those of the suite's catalogue; the mission's family is unfolded first: a brick touched creates the drawer with its key, its title, its message and its fallback time, which you then change like those of any drawer (duration, places…, in its sheet). Under the title of the whole workshop, a line says the model of the mission (« Model: none » for a new mission); « Choose a model », then « Change model », open just below it the list of the models of the mission's family (choose the family in the Frame first): those of the suite (« Blank mission », « Search on a glacier », « Intercepting an aircraft ») then « Your models ». A model poses its drawers and its places « to place », without position, which you then place. If it must replace something, the workshop says so first: « Replaced » (the drawers of the mission, the model's places still to place) and « Kept » (the Frame, the Teams, the Story, the Risks, the Timeline, your places and the model's places you have already placed); « Keep the mission as it is » changes nothing. « Save as a model », on the same line (never in a veiled mission), asks for a name and files the mission among « Your models »: its complete drawers and its places, without any position, without its identifier, its teams or their callsigns; the model is written on the device and goes with the next sending. A model of the same name is replaced after a question (« Replace the model » or « Keep the old one »); the name of a model of the suite is refused. A text the reader refuses (a “<”, a “>” or an address) is not written in the model: the workshop says how many texts were left out. A saved model is not secret: anyone who opens the company’s data repository can read it. The workshop keeps the mission in progress as a draft, on this device only, at each change: nothing is published until you save, and « Leave » does not lose it. One draft per mission (a single one for a mission you create or copy), forty drafts at most (the oldest are erased); resuming it reopens the workshop as you left it, and “Save” makes it the mission and erases the draft. A drawer already written with a hover, a landing or an overflight (the flight judges the hover, the landing and the overflight, over a point or a still object, and « ＋ Drawer » offers them — the pilot of an overflight, once in range, touches “Ask for what comes next”; over a moving object the flight does not judge: the sheet says so, “opens by the gesture or at the fallback time”) shows their fields and can be changed: over a point or following an object of the mission, the radius (hover, overflight), the duration, the tolerated gap in fixes, the speed deviation, the maximum speed of a landing, the heights, the pilot's confirmation for an overflight, and its fallback time; a landing no longer asks for a radius: the sheet says “Judged by tiers: partial 0.5 NM · good 0.2 NM · perfect 0.1 NM · excellent 0.05 NM” (“Judged within 1 NM of the airfield” when it aims at the departure or the arrival), an arrival is judged within 1 NM of the airfield and a stop counts within 2 NM — a radius the file already carries is kept as it is; a value out of its bounds is not written, and “To complete” says what is missing (“condition to complete: Duration (s)”). The event is not offered under “＋ Drawer” as long as the flight does not judge it; the sequence of steps is, and the flight judges it when each of its steps is (no overflight to confirm, no confirmation word, no moving object); the tolerated gap in fixes of a landing or a hover is 5 s at least, and the heights (hover, overflight) are above the ground. A sequence of steps shows its steps in order, one open at a time: its kind (a hover, a landing or an overflight), its name, the fields of its rule and its confirmation word; the others fit on one line, touched to open it; “＋ Step” adds one, ten at most. A drawer awaiting a message from a crew says the identifier of that message and who sends it (“Sent by drawer 1 when it opens”); a drawer that sends one when it opens shows its identifier; its recipients are not typed (the workshop writes “all”, to every crew, until the ergonomist decides) and those a file already carries are kept as they are. The choice of the type offers only what the flight can judge; in the sheet of a drawer, its current key stays there, with its mention. The catalogue also carries bricks of hover, landing, overflight, sequence of steps and message: they appear under “＋ Drawer” only when the flight can judge their key. A drawer already written with a retardant refill (land at a retardant station, stop there, stay, leave again) is edited too: its sheet offers the station — only the “Retardant station” objects of the Places —, the minimum duration in minutes and, if you want, the docking heading, then says the rule in one line (“Land, stop within 15 m of the station, stay 5 min at least, then leave”); the flight judges it, and “＋ Drawer” offers it. A retardant station is not searched for: its sheet in the Places has no detection range, no “found only when the pilot declares it”, no heights.

## Good to know

- The module writes a mission only through its editor (« Create a mission », « Edit », « Duplicate »); the rest of the mission is still written in the CPCO.
- A mission whose file does not come says so: "not found" (the repository has not got it), "not on this device yet" (the synchronisation brings it, the mission appears when it arrives) or "unreadable" (with the reason). Only the registry is shown then.
- A mission that came with the synchronisation without the registry naming it is listed all the same, read from its file, at the next display; no button to press.
- A flight being prepared is replaced only after a question; a started flight never is (cancel the start, or finish the flight, first).
- The envelope, the page-by-page reader and the assignments will come later.

- A closed drawer is not a secret: its content is in the mission file, which every pilot can read. It is a game's seal. Without accounts, a pilot of the mission who writes a false finding in their record opens the drawer for the others: the screen always says by whom, when and how a drawer was opened.

# Missions

The airline's missions, in the Company application: those of the shared repository (written with the CPCO): choose one, or draw one at random, then prepare it in Fuel.

## How to use

<a id="missions-assigned"></a>**My assigned missions.** When the airline's centre assigns you a mission, it appears at the head of Missions, under "My assigned missions": the take-off (aerodrome, time Z), the aircraft, the callsign, and the crew's rendezvous and channels when the assignment carries them ("Open" for a link, "Copy" for a server or a frequency); "Prepare this flight" opens it in Fuel. The group exists only when an assignment carries your trigram; a cancelled assignment, or one whose take-off is more than 12 h past, is no longer there. Assignments are readable by every pilot of the airline: keep session passwords for the channel itself.

<a id="missions-list"></a>**The list.** The missions of the newest series first, grouped by family. Only the missions whose file is on this device are offered, in the list as in the draw; when there is none, the screen says "No readable mission on this device: synchronise". Each row gives the id, the title, the route, the aircraft type and the estimated block; "flown on …" marks a mission whose report you wrote. The search filters on the id, the title, an aerodrome (ICAO code), an aircraft type, a callsign (SAMU66) or the activity (MEDEVAC, HEMS, SAMU…), and also in the content of the missions already on the device: summary, description, objectives, risks, places, notes, callsigns and types, ignoring case, accents and hyphens ("clermont-ferrand" = "Clermont Ferrand"). A place that only a drawer cites is not searched, even once the drawer is open: the search never gives a closed drawer away. When the word is not in the title, the row says where it was found ("in: risks"); a mission whose file is not on the device yet is searched on its registry line, and the list says so ("n missions not read"). Nothing is written or downloaded to search. The registry search is the same; the drop-down list keeps one family, given by its letter (the one of the identifiers), its short name and its name ("T · AIRLIFT · Airlift / logistics"), in letter order; a chosen family with several subtypes opens a second one, "Subtype", beside it. Seven rows are shown, "N more" unfolds the rest. The organisation missions of an exercise (those ending in Z) are not flights and are not listed. Only the missions of the airline chosen in Settings › Airline are listed; a mission that names no airline is personal: it is under « Personnel ». The list « My airline · Personal · All airlines », above the search, also shows the personal missions or those of other airlines, each with the name of its airline; they can be prepared the same way. When the airline has categories (cargo, passengers…), a second drop-down keeps one category, and the open mission says its own.

<a id="missions-open"></a>**Choose, or draw at random.** A tap on a row opens the mission sheet, its Brief: its category, its line of choice (duration · aircraft · theatre, as in the registry), its summary in full under the title "Summary" (beyond 600 signs, "Read more" opens the detail), its risks (the first three, "hazard → mitigation", the others folded, or "No risk declared"), the teams engaged and its zones. "Detail" shows the rest in foldable sections: description (the detailed description only, without the summary), objectives, zones to activate, rendezvous, route provided, maps provided, payload and aircraft; "See on the Map" opens the Map on a zone or on a map of the mission (drawn from its coordinates). On a phone, the browser's back returns from the detail to the sheet, then to the list; on a large screen, the sheet and the detail are side by side. "Draw at random" picks one among the missions of the filtered list you have not flown yet; "Draw again" picks another. Nothing starts by itself: "Prepare this flight" opens Fuel, in the Pilot application, with the departure, the arrival, the points of the mission (as coordinates), the level and the aircraft type; the runway, the procedures, the fuel and the weather are then prepared as for any flight.

<a id="missions-drawers"></a>**The rest of the mission.** A mission may have a sequel that nothing announces: it is given to you in flight. On the sheet of YOUR mission — it is assigned to you, or it is the mission of your flight — it then appears under “Rest of the mission”: its title, how and when (“opened on entering the zone, at 20:10Z”), its message and its briefing; a notification says so. Nothing appears before. “Ask for what comes next”, next to “Prepare this flight”, is touched when you have reached or found what the mission asks for: the centre gives you what comes next if there is anything, otherwise it answers “Nothing new for now”; the command then rests for a minute. During the mission's flight, with a live position (this computer's simulator, the GPS, your online session), your device follows the flight without saying anything; a landing asked for by the mission also counts when your device knows you are on the ground (simulator, IVAO), and so does an overflight, or a hover held within a band of heights above the ground, when it knows it (simulator only), and a retardant refill — down at the station, on the heading asked, for the time asked, then gone again (simulator only); a sequence of steps opens when all are done, in order; without a live position, what comes next arrives at its time. A precision landing on a point is judged in tiers — excellent, perfect, good, partial — which the line of the sequel states with the distance to the target point; for an arrival at an airfield and for a required stopover, the margin is fixed, no longer the radius written in the mission file. What is given to a crew mate on their finding is given to you too — within a few seconds while your sheet is on screen, and the line says “received here at …” when it came later. At the Full level, “Reseal” closes again a part you opened, for you only. As long as a part is not given, the sheet and its Detail neither list nor name what it hides; a mission you are not playing shows only its visible part. The device keeps to itself what was given to you (nothing is sent): the Map uses it. When a part has opened on a precision landing, a block “Debrief” appears under the sequel: one line per landing judged, yours and your crew mates', with the tier and the distance to the target point, computed from your own mission file.

<a id="missions-assign"></a>**Assigning a mission.** At the Everyday level, “Assign…” in the sheet of a mission opens “Assign this mission”: the pilot (a trigram of the airline, or three letters), the callsign, the take-off in UTC and the aircraft — a type of the mission and, if you wish, a registration; the departure aerodrome is the mission's. If the pilot already has a mission less than two hours away, the screen says so and you can assign anyway. The assignments of the mission are listed at the top; “Remove” cancels one. The pilot finds the mission in “My assigned missions”. No one checks rights or availability: they remain with your airline's organisation.

<a id="missions-add"></a>**Add missions.** « Add missions », next to « Draw at random », reads one or more YAML mission files (a file may hold several, separated by « --- » or in a `missions:` list), or a pasted YAML — or an assistant’s whole answer: its code blocks (« ```yaml … ``` », else « ``` … ``` ») are read, each as a source, and the text around them is ignored (the screen says so); an indentation with tabs is refused (YAML is indented with spaces) and an error is given with the line of the pasted text. Before writing, the screen shows what it read (id, title, family, series, airline) and what it refuses, with the reason (id outside the rule, exercise organisation, duplicate, YAML unreadable at a given line). « Add (n) » writes each mission at its place, `missions/<letter>_<FAMILY>/<ID>/mission<ID>.yaml`; a mission that already exists is replaced only when « replace? » is ticked. A mission without an airline is personal. They leave at the next synchronisation. The missions added are entered in the registry at once: they open in the workshop without « Update the registry ».

<a id="missions-airline"></a>**Change airline.** On a mission’s sheet, « Airline: … · Change » offers the airlines and « Personal »; the sentence « … moves from … to … » is said before « Write ». In the list, « Assign to an airline… » takes the missions shown (scope, family, search), each ticked: the screen says how many change and which are unreadable (left as they are) before writing. When adding from YAML, « Add these missions to » chooses their airline. Only the `airline` field of the file changes.

<a id="missions-registry"></a>**The registry.** « Registry », next to « Draw at random » (or the address `#missions?view=registry`), shows one line per mission as the registry `missions/mission_registry.yaml` writes it, with the state of its file: there, missing, unreadable or path refused; and the repository missions the registry does not know. On a computer the registry is a table: the state of the mission (not in the registry, to correct, read, not read — the screen reads 500 files at most and says so in the count), the identifier, the title, the family, the series, the origin and the destination, the duration, then « ⋯ »; each column title sorts (a second tap reverses); on a phone, the same list, one mission per card, and a single « Sort » button that opens the choice of the column and the direction. The default order is the identifier, the most recent on top; the sort chosen is kept on this device, and « Reset the order » goes back to the default. Touching a line opens its sheet, on the right of the table (full screen on a phone, « ← Registry » or the browser's Back returns to the list): the state in words and the file, the route, the family, the series, the number, the operation, the duration, the aircraft and the theatre, then « Brief » (the mission sheet as the pilot sees it, never the content of a closed drawer; « Back to the registry » returns to the same sheet), « Workshop », « Duplicate » and « ⋯ » (« Correct the registry line », « Remove from the registry »). « ⋯ » at the end of a line gives the same gestures without leaving the table. A repository mission the registry does not know (added before the import entered it) has, in its sheet and its « ⋯ », « Enter in the registry »: its line is written from its file alone; opening the registry never writes anything. At the head of the registry, each draft of the workshop kept on this device has its line: « Resume » reopens the workshop as you left it, « Discard » erases it after a confirmation. The line of choice says what the mission file says; a missing duration is computed on the route given, at the cruise speed of the file or at a typical speed of the aircraft's class (helicopter, aeroplane), rounded to five minutes and marked « ≈ »; the theatre is the file's, else the State of the departure aerodrome; what is not known is not shown. The family filter (and its subtype, as in the list) and the search are at the head. « Update the registry » first says what it would do (missions to add, lines without a readable file, duplicate identifiers, former identifiers), then « Write the registry » adds the repository missions, keeps one line per identifier and replaces a former identifier by the folder's; a line without a readable file stays (the file may not have arrived yet). An added mission takes its file's series, else the current series of its family (a number). In an identifier (« A-001A »), the final letter from A to Y is a flight linked to the mission without suffix, Z its organisation. « Correct the registry line » changes the title, series, number or operation of a line; « Remove from the registry » takes it out, the mission file stays in the repository. An unreadable registry is never rewritten; an absent one is created from the files. Nothing, in the registry or the Brief, says that a mission goes beyond its first objective; the box « See the full content of the missions », unticked each time Missions opens and never saved, makes the workshop open the whole mission. It is a secret of the screen: the mission file stays readable by whoever opens it.

<a id="missions-editor"></a>**The editor.** In the registry, « Create a mission » (at the head), « Workshop » and « Duplicate » (in the sheet of a line and its « ⋯ »; « Workshop » also in the Brief opened from the registry) open the editor, which has the shape of a workshop. A mission that exists opens veiled — you too may discover it by playing it: the workshop shows only what the pilot sees at the departure (« At the departure », its places, its route), saying nothing of what might follow; « Reveal the whole mission », in the unfolding, shows everything after a question (« Reveal » or « Keep the secret ») — this button is on every veiled mission, whether it hides something or not. Veiled, the mission is retouched (texts, positions, places added) and saved without losing anything hidden; removing an existing place or changing its identifier asks to reveal it. A mission you create is whole, a copy stays veiled. A saved model is not secret: anyone who opens the company’s data repository can read it. The workshop keeps the mission in progress as a draft, on this device only, at each change: nothing is published until you save, and « Leave » does not lose it. One draft per mission (a single one for a mission you create or copy), forty drafts at most (the oldest are erased); resuming it reopens the workshop as you left it, and “Save” makes it the mission and erases the draft. A drawer already written with a hover, a landing or an overflight (the flight judges the hover, the landing and the overflight, over a point or a still object, and « ＋ Drawer » offers them — the pilot of an overflight, once in range, touches “Ask for what comes next”; over a moving object the flight does not judge: the sheet says so, “opens by the gesture or at the fallback time”) shows their fields and can be changed: over a point or following an object of the mission, the radius (hover, overflight), the duration, the tolerated gap in fixes, the speed deviation, the maximum speed of a landing, the heights, the pilot's confirmation for an overflight, and its fallback time; a landing no longer asks for a radius: the sheet says “Judged by tiers: partial 0.5 NM · good 0.2 NM · perfect 0.1 NM · excellent 0.05 NM” (“Judged within 1 NM of the airfield” when it aims at the departure or the arrival), an arrival is judged within 1 NM of the airfield and a stop counts within 2 NM — a radius the file already carries is kept as it is; a value out of its bounds is not written, and “To complete” says what is missing (“condition to complete: Duration (s)”). The event is not offered under “＋ Drawer” as long as the flight does not judge it; the sequence of steps is, and the flight judges it when each of its steps is (no overflight to confirm, no confirmation word, no moving object); the tolerated gap in fixes of a landing or a hover is 5 s at least, and the heights (hover, overflight) are above the ground. A sequence of steps shows its steps in order, one open at a time: its kind (a hover, a landing or an overflight), its name, the fields of its rule and its confirmation word; the others fit on one line, touched to open it; “＋ Step” adds one, ten at most. A drawer awaiting a message from a crew says the identifier of that message and who sends it (“Sent by drawer 1 when it opens”); a drawer that sends one when it opens shows its identifier; its recipients are not typed (the workshop writes “all”, to every crew, until the ergonomist decides) and those a file already carries are kept as they are. The choice of the type offers only what the flight can judge; in the sheet of a drawer, its current key stays there, with its mention. The catalogue also carries bricks of hover, landing, overflight, sequence of steps and message: they appear under “＋ Drawer” only when the flight can judge their key. A drawer already written with a retardant refill (land at a retardant station, stop there, stay, leave again) is edited too: its sheet offers the station — only the “Retardant station” objects of the Places —, the minimum duration in minutes and, if you want, the docking heading, then says the rule in one line (“Land, stop within 15 m of the station, stay 5 min at least, then leave”); the flight judges it, and “＋ Drawer” offers it. A retardant station is not searched for: its sheet in the Places has no detection range, no “found only when the pilot declares it”, no heights.

# Aircraft

The company's fleet, as the other screens read it: the report (registration → type, callsigns), the map, the CPCO. The performance sheets are in the same screen ("Sheets" tab); the type registry will come.

## How to use it

<a id="aircraft-fleet"></a>**The fleet.** One row per aircraft: registration, type, callsigns, base, "out of service" when it is. The search is the sheets' search plus the fleet: the registration, a callsign, the base, and the type by its code, its name or its maker ("caravelle" finds the aircraft of type S210); under the aircraft, the types that answer are offered ("Caravelle — S210: add an aircraft of this type" opens a new aircraft, the type filled); seven rows are shown, "N more" unfolds the rest. "Add" opens an empty aircraft. The fleet is the one of the airline chosen in Settings › Airline; changing the airline changes the list, and an aircraft added goes into that airline's fleet. When neither aircraft nor type answers, "Create F-BHHH" opens a new aircraft with the typed registration, and "Search on rzjets.net" opens that site in a new tab at this registration (the suite never reads it: come back to enter the type). **The French register.** When the French aircraft register is deposited on this workstation (Data screen), a registration it knows is read under "No aircraft matches": "F-BHHH: CESSNA 172 S (French register, data of …)", the home aerodrome when the register carries it, and the proposed type — a suggestion from the register's label, to check; a type the type database does not recognise is said as it is, never guessed. "Create as registered" opens the filled sheet. In a new aircraft, "Search the French register" lists the registrations of a type (seven, then "n more"); choosing one fills the sheet. The register holds no person's name; nothing is saved before "Save". Without the workstation's launcher (on the phone, on the published site), the register published in the group's data repository answers, with the date of its export, as soon as a workstation has deposited and synchronised it; a maker carried by fewer than ten aircraft stays "not specified". Without the register, a line says so, with the link to Data.

<a id="aircraft-edit"></a>**Editing an aircraft.** A tap on a row opens it: identity (registration, type, variant, owner or operator: an airline, a flying club, a private owner) and operation (callsigns separated by commas, base, tactical number, in service). Under the fields, a line says what will change ("2 fields changed: base, callsigns"); "Save" writes it. From two characters (upper case or not), the Type field suggests the types of the database and the matching sheets ("H145 · Airbus H145 · sheet H145"); a tap, or ↓ then Enter, writes the type code. A type the database does not know is kept as typed, and the screen says so. The Base field is the aerodrome field of Fuel: from two characters it proposes aerodromes (code, name, town or country; helipad list codes such as "LF418" too), the name shows under the field, and a base that is not in the list is kept as typed. The registration is the key: two aircraft cannot share it. The location the site gives stays as it is. "Retire" asks, then the aircraft leaves the fleet; its performance sheet, if it has one, is kept.

<a id="aircraft-sheets"></a>**The sheets.** The "Sheets" tab lists the performance sheets by family (helicopters, turboprops, jets, pistons, others); the search filters on the name, the type or the registration. Each row says where the sheet comes from, what it lacks and its date; a fleet type without a sheet is offered "to complete". "Import a file" reads a CSV, a JSON, a .lnmperf or a SimBrief OFP; "Paste a text" reads a pasted text, a ChatGPT answer included (every value the answer gives then says "ChatGPT" in the sources of its group, even when equal to the one before; the preview says when the answer carries no readable row of RPM / fuel flow table, and each refused row, RPM or fuel flow unreadable); "New sheet" asks for the type code. Nothing is written at once: an import first shows, field by field, what would change; untick what you keep, then "Apply the selection". Several sheets at once: each preview names its sheet and its rank ("3 / 13"), a sheet that does not change is passed by itself, "Skip this sheet" goes to the next one without writing anything, and the end gives the summary; a note already written is replaced only if you tick it. The chosen tab is kept on the device, and the link `#aircraft?sheet=<id>` opens a sheet's editor directly.

<a id="aircraft-sheet-edit"></a>**Editing a sheet.** A tap on its name (or its line) opens the editor: the fields by group (identity, fuel, runway and alternate, climb, cruise, descent, RPM), the RPM / fuel flow table on a large screen, and the EUROCONTROL completion, "Complete from another sheet…" (a button that opens the search of the sheets, nothing chosen in advance) or "Complete with ChatGPT", which copies the request and opens ChatGPT in a new tab: paste the request there if it is not, then paste its answer under the button. "Fuel as volume" and "Fuel density" are explained under the field; "Alternate · go-around (min)" and "Alternate · approach (min)" set the two flat allowances Fuel adds to the alternate (0 to 60 min; empty: 5 and 10 min); a density outside 0.5 to 1.0 is refused. An RPM / fuel-flow table whose flow goes down while the RPM goes up is flagged (the row in orange, "a range misread?"); nothing is corrected without you. A dot "•" at the end of a filled field's label gives its origin and date ("lnmperf · 28/10", "edited · 30/10") on hover or a tap, with the discarded values; each card's title recalls its sources once ("Sources: V2 base · 28/10, edited · 30/10"). "Save" opens the preview of the changes, which are written only after "Apply the selection"; each changed field thus keeps its source, and the old value is kept. "Export .lnmperf" and "Export JSON" download the sheet; "Delete" asks before removing the sheet from the shared database. An altitude in the "ft" column makes a curve of its own: the rows of that altitude; if its flow goes down while the RPM goes up, a line says so with the altitude ("… at 8000 ft: a range misread?") and marks the row, correcting nothing. Under the table, "Propose from the measurements" reads one or more Fuel Phase Monitor journals (.csv, .csv.gz) chosen on the device (they are neither kept nor sent): the steady levels of the sheet's type are listed by altitude and RPM, with the deviation from the curve of the same altitude ("sheet 40, deviation -5 %") or "new row"; tick the ones to keep, "Add the ticked rows to the draft", then "Save" and "Apply" as for any change. With several altitudes, the table is one folding group per altitude ("8000 ft · 4 settings"); each row also has an optional TAS (kt), which Fuel reads when two rows frame the cruise RPM. On the workstation, "From the Output folder" lists the journals of X-Plane's Output folder (set once with "Set the Output folder"): tick them, "Read the ticked journals", and the levels are shown the same way. At the head of the table, "Curves" draws the fuel flow against the RPM, one colour per altitude with its legend; the measured levels are added as squares. The measured climb and descent each have a line there (RPM, fuel flow, rate, airspeed, throttle, and the sheet's current value): ticked, it fills the climb or the descent of the draft. The editor's top line stays visible: the sheet's name and its unit ("lb · lb/h"); a tap on the unit leads to its choice. The levels are put on significant levels (up to 5,000 ft by 1,000, then FL100, 150, 200 … 450: an FL181 counts as FL200); a level without measurement between two measured ones is computed, greyed and said "extrapolated". The measurements accepted stay on the device for a later computation, never published.

<a id="aircraft-fuel-volume"></a>**Fuel as volume.** Ticked: the quantities of this sheet (usable, taxi, reserve, flows) are volumes — litres or gallons, as the simulator shows them —, not masses; the density below converts them to kilograms.

<a id="aircraft-fuel-density"></a>**Density.** Kilograms per litre of the fuel: Jet A-1 about 0.80, Avgas about 0.72. It belongs to the fuel, not to the aircraft: leave it empty for the airline’s value (Settings), else the typical one. Between 0.5 and 1.0.

**Fuel units.** At the top of the editor, "Fuel read and typed in" offers kg, lb, l or US gal (and kg/h, lb/h, l/h, gal/h for the flows); each field and the RPM / fuel-flow table carry the unit chosen. A converted value is written with four significant digits at most, no decimal from three digits before the point, two decimals at most otherwise (12,407.6 lb/h reads 12410; 84.736 reads 84.74; 0.847 reads 0.85); a value you typed stays as you wrote it, and the sheet keeps its value as long as you do not touch the field. The sheet keeps its stored unit: a line says it with the density used; a typed value is converted when saved, an untouched value does not change.

## Good to know

- The Site module also updates the fleet from the pasted "Fleet" page of the site: both write the same file, each save reads the file just before and changes one aircraft only.
- Nothing leaves before the next synchronisation.

# Settings

The settings of this device (⚙ at the top of the screen): your identity, what the app shows you, how data are
exchanged, and what the device keeps offline. They stay on the device, never synchronised.

## How to use

**The status.** The screen opens on the “Status” block, with the same dot as the ⚙ button of the header (green: something
new; amber: attention). One line per thing, touch it to act: the flight in progress (to the Map), the reports to write (to the
pre-filled report), the missing trigram (to “Identity”), the aeronautical database while it is not ready (to “Data
access”), the state of the data and the exchange in use (to “Synchronisation”, or the conflicts), the launcher, “Offline” and,
on a phone, the position during a flight.

**A dot always has its sentence.** When a fold of the Settings carries a dot (yellow: attention; red: urgent), its summary says what is wrong in place of its frequency (“Data access: the token expires in 4 days”) and the card at fault carries the same dot before its title; the ⚙ of the header says the same in its title. A token does not warn before 5 days of its expiry: yellow from 5 to 2 days, red the day before; once expired, the sentence is red at the top of the Settings and under the header, until it is renewed.

**The groups.** At the Discovery level, the screen shows seven cards at most, one under the other: Identity, Data
access, Synchronisation (when it is used), Usages, Level, Design and About. The backup, the offline data, the weather
key, the Little Navmap sheets and the computer access come at the Everyday level. At the Everyday and Full levels the screen is ranged **by frequency of use**: always visible, the “Status” block, the display (usage level, design) and the device's airline; what is used now and then is folded, with its frequency on the right: Data (once a month), Synchronisation (when something is wrong), Device; Administration is a “›” row that opens its own page (with “← Settings” to come back); what is used only once is at the very bottom, folded, under “Getting started” (identity, access, create or join an airline); “About” closes the screen. Touch a folded title to open it. A group that needs attention (a warning,
an error, or the card an amber status line leads to) opens by itself, with an amber dot; a line of the “Status” block
opens the group of its card. A group opened by hand stays open while the page is open. The cards of circumstance (V2
settings found, configuration received, joining resume) come first, at every level, and go once dealt with. A card
opened by its address (the “Offline data” button of the “Join the airline” assistant, for instance) is shown alone,
whatever the level; “← Settings” goes back to the list.

**Your identity.** In “Identity”, type your trigram and your IVAO VID, VATSIM CID and SimBrief IDs, then “Save”. The
tools use them to prefill; with several users, the trigram is needed.

**The level.** “Level” chooses what the menu shows: Discovery, Everyday or Full, with one tap. Under each choice, the
“Menu:” line says which tools it shows; a hidden tool stays reachable by its address.

**The language.** “Language” chooses the language of the whole suite: Automatic (the browser's), Français, English, Deutsch or Español. The choice applies to every screen and the screen reloads at once. It belongs to THIS address of the browser: localhost and 127.0.0.1 are two separate spaces, each keeping its own language.

**The design.** “Design” shows one thumbnail per look — Standard, Cabinet, Rugged — and the tone: Automatic (as the
system), Day or Night. One tap applies it at once, on this device only. A design reserved for the computer does not
apply on a phone: the card says which one is shown instead, and its thumbnail is marked; touching a thumbnail replaces
that choice.

**The usages.** The sentence at the top of “Usages” says how data are exchanged. With no box ticked you are “Alone on
this device”: nothing goes elsewhere. Tick “Alone, on several devices” or “Several users (git data repository)” to
exchange. On a workstation served by the launcher, the app already reads and writes the launcher's `data/` folder, even
with no box ticked; an exchange usage also shares it through git.

**Data access.** On a phone or on the website, set in “Data access” the repository (account/repository), the branch and
your personal token, then “Test” and “Save”. On a workstation served by the launcher there is no token; while `data/` is
not a git repository, the card opens the door to fetch or take it over; its remote repository is set in the
“Administration” card (see “A git repository for data/”). On the launcher's computer, the “Data access” card starts with a “Data repository:” line giving the address and branch of the remote repository: the screen does not wait for it (the launcher may take several seconds to read it), the line shows “…” then its value, without moving; what depends on the repository (the “Remote repository” panel of Administration, for example) appears when it arrives. A request limit that lasts may come from the token: the repository counts requests per user of the token, and a project token often serves several devices. One token per device, or a renewed token, lifts it.

**The GitHub token expiry.** GitHub does not give a page the expiry date: a line of the “Status” block asks you for it, and the optional field “This token expires on” of the “Access” card keeps it. It stays on this device (never in the repository) and you are warned 5 days before, as with GitLab, the date being said “declared”. A token without expiry is told with the box “This token does not expire”: the line goes away. “Cut access” forgets the date.

**No access and no launcher, no bases.** A device with no access set and no launcher — the website opened on a phone
for the first time, for instance — has neither the aeronautical base (airspaces, airfields, beacons) nor the aircraft
base: “Offline data” says so. Two ways in: set the access to the data repository, even without ticking an exchange (it
then only reads the bases); or, on a workstation with the launcher, publish the bases to the repository (V2: Settings ›
Maintenance › Aeronautical data and Aircraft types), then synchronise. Three distinct states when the base is missing: “Aeronautical database loading…” (the read is not over: wait), “Data access not set on this device” (set the access above; an app added to the home screen has its own storage, nothing you set in Safari is there) and “Aeronautical database not published” (the access works, nothing is published in the repository).

**Synchronisation.** The “Synchronisation” card is the summary of what has not left yet: at the top, one count per kind (aircraft sheets, missions, flights, fleet; “Other” only when there is
something), then the last success, the network and the last error. A folded list per kind gives what is waiting: the readable title (for example “Sheet A320 Navy”, “Flight LFPO → LFBO of 3/11”), the date,
✕ for a deletion and, if there is one, the error that holds the queue; seven items visible at most, “… and n more” for the rest. With the workstation's launcher, “changed here” (not yet saved in the
data) and “to send” (saved, not yet sent) are added. “Send everything” sends and receives at once; otherwise synchronisation runs by itself at start, every 10 minutes online and when the network comes
back. The card appears as soon as an exchange is ticked or something is waiting. Below it, one line says what has just left (“Sent at 18:04: Aircraft sheets: 2 · Missions: 1”, with “of which 1 refused” if any); unfolded, it gives the files one by one by their readable title, the refusals first with the reason the transport gave, seven at most. It is the last synchronisation that sent something: a pass with nothing to send leaves it as it was, and nothing is said before the first send. On a workstation with a launcher, two lines say **how long the synchronisation took**: the last one and **the longest of today** (kept on this device), in seconds, step by step (settle, commit, receive, push…), with the number of git commands and what was sent (“8 s — commit 1 s · receive 2 s · push 4 s · 12 git commands (1 s) · 38 Ko sent”); a shorter pass does not replace the longest, another day clears it.

**The backup.** Alone on this device, “Export (one file)” keeps all your data in a file: it is the only copy. “Import
(merge)…” takes it back without losing anything here; “Replace from a file…” first erases what is on the device.

**Start again from scratch.** When something looks odd, “Start again from scratch on this device” (the “Device” group, and a line of the “Status” block leads to it) empties the copies of the repository files that the device can read again, then reads them again. It says the figures first, asks one question, and sends what waits first. Never your waiting sends, your settings, your drafts or your documents. With no network, or if the repository does not answer, nothing is emptied: the device would keep no bases until the network came back.

**Offline data.** Each published zone has its row: its state on this device, its cycle and its size. “Keep offline”
downloads it whole (preferably on Wi-Fi); “Free” gives the space back.

## A git repository for data/

**GitHub or GitLab?** It is not a setting: the provider is read from the remote repository’s address and shown at the top of “Administration › Remote repository of data/”; the “Data access” card leads there.

On a workstation served by the launcher, at the Full level, the “Remote repository of data/” button of the
“Administration” card shows the remote repository of `data/`: its address and its branch. To set or change it, type the address (`git@host:account/repo.git` or `https://…`, with no password or token
in it): “Check” says whether the repository answers, “Save” sets it. Towards another address than the configured one, a Yes / No window opens on the computer, not in the page: the card says so before the click; answer it (it may be behind other windows), then come back. Refused, closed or already open: the card gives the reason and `data/` stays as it is. This is on purpose: a malicious page must not be able to send your data elsewhere. Nothing blocks: a repository that does not answer is
only reported.

- **Access to the repository** is set on the computer: an SSH key for a `git@…` address, credentials stored in Git for

- **The provider** of a configuration is chosen (github or gitlab), never guessed; the panel only reads the remote repository's address (GitHub, gitlab.com, framagit.org, or a self-hosted GitLab that the device's configuration names with the same host; otherwise “Provider not recognised”). The page only suggests: changing the repository goes, as always, through the launcher's Yes / No window.   the “Usages” card says so.
- **data/ missing or without git**: the “Data access” card opens a door, at every level. `data/` is a folder ignored by the code repository; it
  becomes its own git repository, linked to the data repository (not a submodule). Type the address of the data

  “Take data/ into this repository” links it to the repository. The card follows the work and reads the state again at
  the end; a failure is explained, nothing is lost.
- **Rewritten repository history** (for instance to purge big files from it): this computer's synchronisation then
  refuses any merge and says so; nothing is lost. Here, « Take up the new history » first shows what will leave (your
  commits, the data files carried over), asks, keeps the old state in a local reference, carries your data files onto
  the new history, then synchronises. A file outside the synchronised data is never sent again.

## Configuring a pilot

**Preparing the configuration (administrator).** In the “Administration” card (“Full” level), “Add a pilot” prepares a pilot’s configuration (see “Add a pilot (administrator)” below). It gives **three forms of the same configuration**: the file sog-config-<TRI>.json, the link and its QR code; send whichever you like. The pilot scans the QR code with their phone’s camera: it opens the link, which leads to the assistant. They contain the airline, the data repository, the trigram and the token how-to, never a token or a secret; the repository’s owner (user or organisation) is noted there, it decides which token to create. For a GitHub repository, first invite the pilot’s GitHub name on the repository, with the Write right (the link leads to the repository’s collaborators page).

**Joining the airline (pilot).** The QR code (scanned with the phone’s camera: it opens the link), the link, “Join the airline…” (“Data access” card: the assistant without a configuration asks you to paste the link received, or to choose the file) and the welcome card of the first opening open the “Join the airline” assistant, one screen per step: the
configuration received; the account (create it if needed, with the sign-up link) and the username; the invitation to
accept; the token, step by step (classic for a user's repository, fine-grained for an organisation's); the token pasted
and tested; the network IDs; and last “Finish and synchronise”, then the offline data. Ticked boxes stay on the device:
a settings card offers to resume where you left off. The “Join the airline” tutorial follows the same steps.

**Joining for a GitLab project (pilot).** When the configuration you received is a GitLab project, you create nothing: no account, no invitation, no token. The assistant has only three screens: the configuration read (airline, project), then “Paste the token” — paste the token the administrator sent you apart (never in the link); your trigram is read from the configuration, you do not type it — then “Finish and synchronise”. Your VID, CID and SimBrief identifiers are filled in later in Settings › Identity. Before accepting the token, the app asks GitLab what kind of token it is. A personal token (it works for all your projects) gives a question: prefer the project token asked from the administrator; if you answer “No”, nothing is saved. A token without an expiry date (or more than a year away), or with more rights than needed, is flagged with a warning line, without blocking you. The “Data access” card says the same of a personal token, without a question: alone with your own repository, a personal token is the right one.

**Nothing more than the file.** Only what the configuration holds is set, and the access is saved only once the token is
tested; “Test” says what is wrong: invitation not accepted yet, token copied wrongly or expired, token without the right
on the contents. A classic token opens all the repositories of the account: for a user's repository, a GitHub account
dedicated to flying is advised. A file in another format is refused with a message. The airline is written to the
repository only if it has none yet. On a workstation whose data/ already follows this repository, the assistant skips
the account and the token. Without a launcher, the app reaches a GitHub repository or a project on a GitLab instance
(gitlab.com): paste the project's address (https://gitlab.com/group/project) and the project access token the
administrator gave you; the token is sent to that instance only. When the received configuration or the device's access is a GitLab project, the “Data access” card expects the project's address and describes the project token (Developer role, `write_repository`).

on a workstation served by the launcher, the “Remote repository of data/” and “Airline API keys (IVAO, EAD Basic)” buttons, which

**The administrator’s assistant.** The “Administrator’s assistant” button (“Administration” card, or the address `#settings?card=assistant`) takes the administrator by the hand: six steps, in order, each with its state read from the installation (done, to do, optional, unknown), a sentence and a button to the card that sets the step. The first step to do has the coloured button. The data repository (a remote repository for data/ and, for GitLab, your administrator token on this workstation), the airline (its name, in the Website module; your trigram is in Settings › Identity), the aeronautical base (at least one published zone), the first pilot (“Add a pilot”), then, optional, the devices and the keys (IVAO, EAD). The state of the installation, as the frame gives it, is at the bottom. The assistant writes nothing: each button opens the card that already exists.

**Add a pilot (administrator).** At the “Full” level, the “Add a pilot” button of the “Administration” card (where the export is possible) prepares everything for a pilot who has nothing yet. Type their trigram, their name if you want to greet them, the language of the message and the published app’s address, then “Prepare”: you get the file, the link and the QR code (three forms of the same configuration), your steps, and a message ready to copy, in the chosen language. For a GitLab repository, your steps are: create the pilot’s project token (one per pilot, name = their trigram, role Developer, scope `api`, expiry date 90 days advised and 365 at most, never without a date), copy it at once (GitLab shows it only once), check that the data branch lets Developers push, and know how to revoke (Settings › Access tokens › Revoke). With the launcher, “Create their token” opens the “Pilots’ tokens” screen with the trigram already typed: the launcher creates the token with the administrator’s; the steps by hand stay folded below. Without a launcher, they are open. **Send the token apart from the message** (another channel, or paste it in front of the pilot): it is never in the link or in the message. For a GitHub repository, the steps are the export’s.

## Good to know

- **IVAO account**: the « IVAO account » card links this device to your IVAO account through the airline's relay (or
  the workstation's launcher): « Sign in to IVAO » opens IVAO's page, then comes back here. The relay keeps the airline
  application's secret; your token stays on this device, renewed by itself, never in a backup. « Unlink » erases it and asks IVAO
  to withdraw the access; if IVAO cannot, the suite tells you to withdraw it in your IVAO account. Without an IVAO
  application set, the button is off and the card says where it is missing: on this workstation, Administration › « IVAO
  application » sets it (it gives the return address to declare at IVAO); on the relay, the administrator sets it.
- **Devices**: one screen, Maintenance › « Devices », for every pilot's device, whether it comes from the local network
  or the airline's relay (when the launcher runs as the relay, behind the NAS's reverse proxy, in https). A pilot pairs
  each device once with an 8-digit code the administrator asks for there for their trigram (once, 10 minutes, on either
  channel); their writes are signed with that trigram. Each row says the device's channel; a device paired with the
  console's code says « console code », and « Name » sets a trigram on it. A lost device, or all those of a pilot who
  leaves, are revoked there.
- **Update the suite** (Administration, with the launcher): choose a channel — stable, pre-releases, or development
  follow-up —, “Check” says what would change without touching anything, then “Update” (after a question). Never a step back;
  refused if files of the suite were changed on this computer, and while a job of the Data module or a synchronisation runs
  (try again once it is over). The data are not touched. The version taken is the one the suite’s repository announces: its
  tags are not signed, the trust is the same as an update made by hand with git. It is the one “Check” showed: if the
  channel changed meanwhile, nothing is written and the panel asks to check again.
  Then close the launcher’s window and run
  lancer.cmd again.
- **Folders of this computer** (Administration, with the launcher): where the suite, the data, the downloads, Little
  Navmap's databases and sheets are on this computer, the path read only and where it comes from; “Open” shows the folder
  in the file explorer. A path is changed where it says, never here; X-Plane is unknown to the launcher.
- **Pilots' tokens (GitLab)**: in the Maintenance application, on the launcher's computer, « Pilots' tokens » creates
  one project access token per pilot (named after the trigram, Developer role, one year at most) and revokes it. After
  « Create the token », the screen shows only that token, at the top: copy it at once (it will not be shown again), with
  what to tell the pilot and the link to their message (« Add a pilot »); the « Existing tokens » list is folded below,
  the new one there « created just now ». The launcher uses the administrator's token there, which it never shows: a personal access token of your
  GitLab account (Preferences › Access tokens), scope api, with an expiry (one year at most), this account being
  Maintainer or Owner of the project. Paste it in « Administrator's token », under the list; « Test the administrator's
  token » says the account, its role, its scopes and its expiry; the block's title says whether a token is set (its date, the
  instance, the project, the time of the last test), never its value. A refusal of GitLab is said by its cause: token refused
  (missing, expired or without api), account that is not Maintainer, project not found. The data branch must be protected,
  open to the Developer role and without force push (on gitlab.com the default branch is reserved to Maintainers): the screen checks it, and « Open the
  branch to Developers » does it after a question, asked too before the first token. A pilot whose token is refused
  writing reads « the project's branch main is not open to Developers ».
- **Token expiry (GitLab)**: the « Existing tokens » list says, for each token, « expires on … · in 12 days » (nothing before 5 days, amber from 5 to 2 days, red the day before, « expired on … » once the date is past); the date is read from GitLab each time the screen opens, nothing of it is kept. On the launcher's computer, the « Status » block of Settings says « 2 pilot tokens expire soon (nearest: …) »: the number and the nearest date, read at most once a day, never a name. For its own token, the device keeps only the expiry date GitLab gives (never the token), reads it again at most once a day, says it in the « Status » block and, under 5 days (red the day before; once expired, the sentence is at the top of Settings and under the header), once a day in a notification. A refused token is said « expired on … » only when that known date is past: GitLab answers an expired, a revoked and an unknown token the same way. With GitHub, no date is read (the browser cannot read the header that carries it).
- **The token**: on github.com, a fine-grained token, on the data repository only, “Contents” read and write, 90 days
  at most; on GitLab, a project access token (one per pilot, named after the trigram), created by the administrator. It
  is never shown again; never paste it in a message.
- **Your settings stay after the browser is closed**: the identity, the token and the weather key are kept in the same place
  on the device, never synchronised nor in a backup. If Safari made you lose them anyway (it clears the storage of a site
  that is little used), add the app to the Home Screen: it is better kept there. The diagnostics (“Copy”) say whether the
  browser promised not to clear (`storage.persisted`).
- **Cut access**: erases the token from this device; your pending writes stay and will leave once access is set again.
  Lost or stolen phone: also delete the token at the host (github.com, gitlab.com…).
- **V2 settings**: if this browser was already used for V2, the “V2 settings found” card offers once to take over your
  identity and your access.
- **About**: links to this help and to the roadmap (what is delivered, in progress, planned), then the
  diagnostics, folded: transport, last sync, errors, repository tree, reference bases and aeronautical base, line by
  line; “Copy” puts it all on the clipboard, to paste in your message to report a problem. The token is
  never in it.

**The airline.** At the “Full” level (or through #settings?card=company), the “Airline” card lists the airlines of data/airline/index.json and “Personal”: the name, the code, the networks (IVAO, VATSIM, off-line) and the number of aircraft in the fleet. The card is titled “This device’s airline”: a list of radio buttons, one line per airline (the name, the code, the kind said in words — “Commercial”, “SO squadron” or “Personal” —, the networks and the fleet). Touching a line makes it the current airline of this device (the choice is per device): only the radio buttons change, the list is not redrawn. While the frame cannot yet save this choice, the buttons are grey and the card says so. Under the list, the sheet of the chosen airline (the others have none), open: the **kind** (two radio buttons, a sentence each) then the **mission families** (one box per family: the acronym and the name), ticked from the kind while nothing has been ticked; ticking or unticking writes themes.json, at least one family stays ticked. **Add a family** (an acronym of 2 to 8 letters or digits, a name of 1 to 40 characters; eight at most; a free **letter** is proposed to number the family's reports, refused with its reasons when a family of the table, M, D, X or another added family already has it; a family added before the letters gets a free letter proposed, written only when you set it) and **Add a series** (a family of the list, a name; twenty per family): the name typed in the language of the screen is copied into the four languages; the family or series is removed with “Remove”. Three gestures follow, in this order: “Create an airline” opens an **assistant on the same screen**: the **Identity** step (the name; the code, proposed from the name and editable, 2 to 8 lower-case letters or digits, never one already taken; the **kind**, Commercial or SO squadron, Commercial preselected; the networks), then the **Flight families** step (the kind's own, ticked in advance: untick what you do not fly; nothing is written for the families if you do not touch them), then the **Fleet** step (“Fleet · 2 aircraft”: search a type in the sheets base, type the registration, “Add the aircraft”; “Remove”; “Later” if you prefer to add the aircraft from Aircraft), then the **Summary** in words (“Rennes Motoculture · Commercial · IVAO · 1 family · 2 aircraft”). “Back” keeps everything typed. **Nothing is written before “Create the airline”**: then the settings, the (empty) fleet and, last, the entry in the list (an airline is listed only when complete); an existing file is never overwritten. If the creation is interrupted, the message says what is done and “Resume” finishes without overwriting anything. At the end, “Fly for this airline” chooses it and opens Missions. If the code typed already has its folder but the airline is missing from the list, the assistant says so and offers **“Take this airline back”**: it goes back into the list, nothing else is written. Twenty airlines at most; everything read from data/airline/ is treated as untrusted text. “Join an airline” opens another assistant: paste the “Fleet” page of the airline’s website (or “Read the clipboard”), then “Read the page”; the screen shows **what was read** (“4 aircraft · 3 operators”), the name and code proposed, the kind (“SO squadron” by default), the networks and the address of the site (optional, https) **before anything is written**; “Join” then writes the settings, the fleet read and, last, the entry in the list. The “what was read” step also says **how many missions of the registry are already at this code** (“Registry: 2 missions already at this code”), and follows the code you type. An airline already in the list is not joined again: its fleet is refreshed by the paste of the airline website screen. On the launcher's computer, while your airline is still at the root of data/airline/, the card offers to **migrate** it: an optional code (proposed from the name), “See what would be done” (the name, the folder data/airline/<code>/, the files and their size, the fleet that will be empty there), then “Migrate to “<code>””. Nothing is deleted or overwritten: the current files stay in place as a copy. In the sheet: with no kind every family is ticked in advance and the sheet asks you to choose the kind; a commercial airline has the Commercial family (cargo, passengers, empty leg, VIP, training, transit, test flights), a squadron the sixteen military families. “Personal” (every family) has nothing to set.
