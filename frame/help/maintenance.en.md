# Maintenance — manual

The Maintenance app is for the airline's administrator: the data it publishes for the pilots (Data module), the fleet and
its performance sheets (Aircraft module), and the suite's administration (Settings, Administration card). It opens at the
Full level. With the workstation's launcher it writes; without a launcher, or from a paired device, it is read only.

## First start

- **The entries.** On a phone, the bottom bar carries Maintenance's modules: Data, and Sheets (performance sheets of the types) at the level that opens
  it. Top right, as in Pilot, "?" opens this help and ⚙ the Settings, the Administration card included. On a screen they
  are the entries of the left column, with Administration and the workstation's service pages (pilots' tokens,
  devices, V2 maintenance page). The application's name, top left, opens the list of applications: one tap goes to Pilot.
- **The help.** One door, the "?": it opens at the chapter of the screen shown, with the contents and "All help"
  (the manuals and the tutorials) at the head; this manual covers Maintenance only, Pilot's stays in Pilot.
- **The app's status.** The dot of the ⚙ (green: something new; orange: something needs your attention) leads to the
  “Status” block at the top of Settings.

# Data

What the airline publishes for the app — the aeronautical base, the procedures, the aircraft, the weather — and the state of each data set. This screen is for reading, and offers actions on every group: Download the sources, Build, Purge, then Publish.

## How to use it

<a id="data-cycle"></a>**The cycle in force.** At the top, the current AIRAC cycle (for example 2610), its dates and the number of days before the next one. Below, "Launcher of this workstation" or "No launcher: read only" says whether this computer has a launcher.

<a id="data-coverage"></a>**The coverage.** The list of zones (countries, regions) this workstation follows. On the first opening it holds every zone the airline publishes; the list is kept on this device. “Add a country” puts into the coverage a zone the suite knows, even one nobody publishes yet: it is ticked for “Build”.

<a id="data-packs"></a>**Country packs.** Beside the map, with the list of zones (under it on a phone), when AeroDB gives packs: one row per regional pack (Western Europe, Asia…) with its number of countries and its size. The row is a **“the whole pack in the coverage” switch**: on, all its zones are in the coverage (ticked); half, some are; turning it on adds the missing ones; **turning it off removes all its zones from the coverage**, after a question that names them: they are unticked and leave this workstation's list, **nothing is purged** (their files stay). Hovering a row with the mouse, or pressing it on a phone, **lights its zones on the map**. Under the switch, a folded **“Countries (n)”** lists the pack's countries with a tick each (ticked = in the coverage): ticking a country adds it alone, nothing is downloaded or built; open, it scrolls inside itself. An empty pack says why (“no zone and no source today”). Regions and **themes** (islands, deserts…) share one block with two tabs, “Regions” and “Themes”. A theme is a fold that shows its countries **in the same grid as the list of zones**: one tick per country (ticked = in the coverage), the name; ticking a country adds it to the coverage, unticking it removes it after a question (nothing is purged); at the head of the grid, a “The whole theme” switch. One button only, **“Update (n)”**, on the row of a pack of which n countries are in the coverage: the update path (download, state, then one question) on those countries only, without touching what is ticked in the list; the countries of an open fold are indented under their pack.

<a id="data-states"></a>**The States.** Under the coverage, the States are sorted by colour: green, procedures are already published; orange, they can be read on demand; red, nothing is readable. The reason is written on each line, the colour is never alone. Beyond seven lines, "n more" unfolds the rest.

<a id="data-group-base"></a>**Aeronautical base.** One line per source that feeds a zone of the coverage: its cycle, its age ("published 3 days ago"), the number of zones published and a state badge. Tap a line to see the source's link with its address, its licence, the zones it feeds and what each zone has published.

<a id="data-group-proc"></a>**Procedures.** The procedures of France (SIA eAIP) and of the United States (FAA CIFP), with the number of aerodromes published. Each State line also says the aerodromes whose procedures are already here (“France — 3,069 aerodromes, cycle 2611”; “n aerodromes read” for the other States).

<a id="data-group-aircraft"></a>**Aircraft.** The aircraft types and their performance: the edition date, the number of types and of files. After 28 days the badge turns orange.

<a id="data-group-weather"></a>**Weather.** The world weather and the NAT tracks are published with the website: these lines have neither a cycle nor a badge.

<a id="data-world"></a>**World synoptic (GFS).** In the Weather group, one row for the world synoptic forecast of the Atmosphere map: its state (“Not on this workstation” until one is published, “Over 12 h old” as it ages), the GFS run, the number of steps, its weight and its age. “Build” makes it on the workstation (about 2 minutes the first time; a few seconds if the run is already there, it is then reused). It is published **locally**, in data/weather/world/: never synchronised nor committed. A failure says its reason (no run published or NOAA unreachable, invalid folder, time exceeded) and the old publication stays.

<a id="data-zone-link"></a>**Links to a zone.** `#data?zone=<id>` opens the base, ticks that zone alone and brings “Update” on screen; a zone that is not in the defined countries is said, with “Add a country”. The reasons of a zone to rebuild also count: file of the cycle absent, built before a source's file was there, file present but the “personal” box not ticked (rebuild with the box), file to drop. A **notice** (“zone without points or airways: the source does not carry them”) does not make the zone stale.

<a id="data-states-pill"></a>**The badges.** "Up to date": nothing to do. "Cycle out of date": the published cycle is older than the cycle in force (orange, red after a week). "Old parser": published by an older version of the builders. "Over 28 days old": a set without a cycle, published more than 28 days ago. "Not in the repository": nothing is published. A group opens by itself when one of its lines needs attention.

<a id="data-more"></a>**The other data sets.** At most seven lines per group; "n more data sets" unfolds the rest and the sources that are only considered or set aside (their link, licence and status).

<a id="data-build"></a>**Build the base.** At the top of the Aeronautical base group, the "Update the base" panel acts on the ticked zones with three buttons, in this order: Download · Build · Purge. "Build" rebuilds the base of the ticked zones on this workstation's launcher: a windowless browser (Edge, else Chrome) builds the zones one after the other, and each one is saved to the data repository as soon as it is ready. Every zone of the coverage is ticked at the start; untick the ones you do not want to redo. The three buttons go grey together, with their reason written once below: "The workstation launcher is needed", "Another operation is running", "Tick at least one zone", "Offline". When the launcher refuses because another operation is running, a **card** says which one (its name, its UTC start time, where it is, alive or "not answering") with "View" (back to its follow-up), "Stop" and, only when it no longer answers, "Release" (the launcher checks before lifting the lock); a file sent and refused for the same reason shows the same card.

<a id="data-build-personal"></a>**Personal-licence sources.** ENAIRE (Spain), NATS (United Kingdom), AirNav (Ireland) and the X-Plane data are listed when a ticked zone uses them. **One box** unlocks them all: “I certify that I use the data for my personal use” (never ticked by default: tick it only if the data repository is used by you alone). Ticked, it is remembered on this workstation, found ticked at each opening of Data, and goes out with every “Download”, “Build” and “Update” (every personal-use source of the ticked zones); only its own box undoes it. With a launcher on this workstation, **the launcher holds the tick** (data/local/personal_use.json): at the opening the box shows ITS file, each click rewrites it, and the tick of this page, if it was already set, is handed to it once; if the launcher refuses, the box goes back as it was and says so. This protects against forgetting and against page code that would declare the tick for you; a script of the same origin could, however, call the same route. What is proprietary (X-Plane, EUROCONTROL) stays **read on this workstation only**: never in the published base nor in an export, ticked or not. The “Sources and licences” link opens the page that lists each source (publisher, licence, credit, date). An old per-source box that was ticked becomes the one box. **A zone built with one of them is published in your data repository**; only X-Plane and EUROCONTROL stay read on this workstation, never published.

<a id="data-build-run"></a>**While it builds.** A bar, "n of m zones", the step and the elapsed time; one line per zone says waiting, building (n / m sources), built, failed with its reason, or cancelled. "Stop" asks for the stop: the zone in progress finishes, the zones already built are kept. You can leave the screen: the build goes on in the launcher and is found again when you come back. If the launcher stopped on the way, the screen says "Build interrupted" with the last state of each zone: start again, unticking the zones already done if you like.

<a id="data-build-end"></a>**At the end.** A notification gives the counts ("Base built: 3 zones; 1 failed (gb)") and the summary stays in the panel with what to do: a failed zone (the reason is written on its line: windowless browser, time limit, invalid output, could not write…), a stopped build, no browser found ("Neither Edge nor Chrome was found on this workstation: install one of them."). The state of the lines is read again afterwards. When a source of the base is of a past cycle (the SIA at 2610 while 2611 is in force), one orange line names it once, with its page and the file to take: "Build" would rebuild the old cycle. A zone without a dated source (openAIP, GeoNames, relief) is never said to be out of date. When the cycle in force of a source to take by hand is missing, an orange line under the buttons says so ("SIA France 2611 missing: download export_xml_bd_SIA_<date>-v<nn>.zip from the SIA site"); "Take the downloaded file" then becomes the main button and "Build" loses it. When a take leads nowhere, the reason is written **on the zone's line** (what was found in Downloads, what is missing, what is refused) and stays there until the next attempt; "Choose the file…" is offered on that line as soon as it carries a message or waits for a file; a greyed button says why. A hand-taken source (NAV Portugal: a zip carrying the ISO, the ISO alone, a folder) offers "Choose the file…" from the start, with its steps; when a file is seen in Downloads, "Take the downloaded file" is the main gesture and "Choose another file…" a link under it; the line says the edition read ("edition of 1 October 2026"), whether the file is the previous cycle's or a future edition, and each refused file with its reason. The list of sources the upload takes comes from the launcher alone. That line comes from the last download or build (the journal keeps it): nothing is started to read it, and "Cycle X out of date" names the source at fault.

<a id="data-update"></a>**Update.** The main button of the base panel: it downloads the sources of the ticked zones (like “Download”), then reads the **state of each zone** from the launcher and rebuilds only those that are not up to date and that the build can handle. A single question names those zones, the reason for each (one sentence when there are many: “45 zones: built with an older parser”) and their size, and says it may take a while; if nothing needs rebuilding, a sentence says so. The zones that wait for a file from Downloads are named: “Take the downloaded file” first. If the state cannot be read, nothing is rebuilt. “Update” is the only button in view; **“Detail”** (folded) holds “Download”, “Take the downloaded file” (which stays in view, as the main button, when a manual cycle is missing), “Rebuild all” (the former build, on the whole selection whatever its state: after a purge, or when in doubt) and “Purge”. Beyond 60 ticked zones the verbs work in runs of 60, one after the other; “Purge” first plans each run, then asks **one single question** that gives the sum. Each zone of the list and of the map says its state, as the launcher gives it: **green** (up to date), **orange** (to rebuild: touch the pill to read why — newer source, source files changed, older parser, missing files, a file to take…), **grey** (never built), **red** (the last build failed), **blue** (the page is building it). Zones are **independent**: a download or a run of 60 zones that fails does not stop the others, the failed zone is a line with its reason. At the top of the base panel, a **state sentence** (“Cycle 2611, in force since … Your base: 58 zones up to date · 2 to rebuild · 2 failed · 1 waits for a file · 3 without state”): a zone without state is counted; each number is a button that unfolds, right below, the zones it counts (with their box, ticking nothing, no scrolling). Under it, **the** button “Update (n)” (the whole coverage) or “Update the selection (n)” (at least one zone ticked and at least one unticked), and the line of what it will do (“download 3 zones, rebuild 2 zones”); the progress and the final summary come right below. All up to date: “Everything is up to date.”, no button. “Everything is up to date.” is said only when nothing is left to rebuild, failed, waiting for a file or for the tick; otherwise the line says what waits for your hand. A zone whose source is under a personal licence and whose tick is missing offers no file: its line says to tick “I certify…” (link to the box) and the state sentence counts them apart (“wait for the tick”). A remark from the launcher (“no points or airways: the source does not carry them”) is information, never a state. The state word of a line (“To rebuild”) opens its reason (“why?”) without moving the list; a reason the page does not know is shown by its code. The map legend has three states: up to date, to do, failed. On the line of a failed zone, **“Try again”** (the build of that zone alone); on the line of a zone waiting for a file, what it waits for and **“Take the downloaded file”**, which then starts the build of the zone. **Update only does what is not up to date**: the state of every zone is read **before** anything is downloaded; a zone that is up to date is skipped, a zone whose sources of the cycle are already there is **not downloaded again** (its build only), and a line says what is going to be done (“1 zone to download, 3 zones to rebuild, 2 zones already up to date”). When a source refuses the download (HTTP 403 for instance), the zone says which one and what it answered (“FAA: download refused (403)”) with **“Try again”** on its line, which replays the download of that zone alone; the other zones go on. “Rebuild all” stays the gesture that redoes everything. **Beyond 60 zones** the state is read in runs of 60 and merged: a coverage of 78 zones was read for the first 60 only, the others had no pill and no proposal to rebuild. When the **FAA** refuses the automatic download (United States, HTTP 403), the zone’s line also offers **“Choose the file…”** and the direct link to the NASR subscription page, where to take the three sets of the cycle (FIX, AWY, NAV). When the FAA refuses, drop the **three NASR sets** by hand (FIX, AWY, NAV, with the FAA's original file names): each recognised upload is counted, the screen says what is missing, and at the third the zone's download reads them and the build starts. The **Country packs** block is folded when the screen opens. A tap on “n to rebuild” (or “failed”) **ticks those zones**, shows them alone, brings the list into view and says in one line what to do.

<a id="data-select"></a>**The map, the country groups and a country's sheet.** Under the state sentence comes **the map** (the country outlines, framed on your defined countries; three states: up to date, to do, failed; a hatch where procedures exist; “See the whole world”), then **the country groups** (one line per group: “12 of 14 up to date · 2 to do”; only one group opens at a time, its countries get a blue border on the map, the hovered country fills in alone; the group's “Update”). Touching a country, on the map or in a group, opens **its sheet** (right of the map on a wide screen, full screen with “← Back” on a phone): its state, the date of its build and, for each source, the three ticks put by the tool alone (downloaded · built · published, with the date and the edition); for a hand-taken source, the inventory of its files in three beats (what it is, where to find it, the expected name) with those seen in Downloads and those missing. A source unavailable at its publisher is set apart. There is no flat list of zones and no selection any more: the button at the top acts on the whole coverage, a group's on its countries. On the sheet one acts: “Update this country” (it says what it will do, and why it is greyed); for a hand-taken source, “Take the downloaded file” is the main gesture when a file is seen in Downloads, “Choose another file…” a link under it, and “Choose the file…” alone when nothing is seen (the SIA zip for DIRCAM: the page reads only the useful member, without loading the zip). Between the map and the groups, **“what is left to do this month”** (“AIRAC cycle 2611: 12 done out of 15”): one line per source not done, with its three ticks put by the tool (downloaded · built · published, the date or the reason in words), leading to the country's sheet; the sources already done are folded, those unavailable at their publisher are set apart and do not count. The **wheel** zooms the map wherever the pointer is, over a country's outline included (at the end of the zoom it scrolls the page again); **hovering a group** of the grid outlines its perimeter in blue on the map without framing it (the framing stays with the click); a country picked with Shift + click stays outlined in the same blue as long as it is picked. The **defined countries** (“the coverage” in the specs and in the code) are the countries whose base this workstation builds: one is added from its sheet (“Add this country”) or several from the banner of the map (“Add these countries (n)”), one is removed from its sheet (“Remove this country”). **Why a country is not green**: under the state sentence, one line per kind names the countries that are not up to date (to rebuild, failed, waiting for a file, waiting for the personal-use tick, state not read), with the reason when they all share it; the tooltip of an amber or red country on the map gives it too. A country whose state is not read yet is **grey with a dashed outline** (“state not read”), never amber: a read in progress does not look like a fault. A notice from the launcher (“zone without points or airways”) is information, never a colour. **A country's sheet says ONE thing to do now**, at the top, under the state: take the file seen in Downloads, deposit the expected file, tick “I certify…”, “Update this country”, or nothing (up to date); the button at the bottom says the same, never the opposite. A source the launcher reads on the site but whose file is taken by hand (Portugal's ISO disk) is shown as a hand-taken source. During an operation (the update of a country, of a group, of the base) the country being processed is **blue “in progress”** from start to end, on the map, in its group and in the group's summary (“1 in progress”): never the amber of “to do” between two steps.

<a id="data-download"></a>**Download.** The "Download" button fetches, on this workstation's launcher, the raw sources of the ticked zones (openAIP, FAA, UK Mil AIP, NAV CANADA, today's NAT tracks; ENAIRE only if it is ticked among the personal-licence sources). One line per zone says where it stands ("downloading (2 / 3 sources)") and, below it, one line per source: downloaded (n files), unchanged, failed with its reason and HTTP code ("HTTP 404"), skipped (licence not declared) or manual. What has not changed is never rewritten. At the end a notification gives the counts ("Downloaded: 2 zones changed, 1 unchanged; 1 failure"); the sources the launcher does not download (the SIA, for example) are listed by name, with a link only if their address is https, "to be dropped in by hand"; if source files tracked by git were modified, the screen says so ("n source files changed, outside the synchronised data"). "Nothing to download for these zones": none of the ticked zones has a downloadable source. Two downloads cannot follow each other within 30 s: the screen says "Try again in n s".

<a id="data-zone-map"></a>**The map of the zones to tick.** Above the list of zones (base panel) and of States (procedures panel: France and the United States), a real map (the application's shared map base, as the Map and the Weather use) draws the zones filled with the tint of their state: you pan and zoom it with the finger, and the "Recenter" button brings the view back to all the zones. Tapping a zone ticks or unticks it, exactly like its box in the list (one selection: the map and the list are both redrawn from it); dragging or pinching the map ticks nothing. A country the suite knows but that is not in your defined countries is drawn in grey (“not defined”): tapping it adds it to the coverage and ticks it, like “Add a country”; tapping it again unticks it. A country of the coverage is only ticked or unticked: a tap never removes it. With the keyboard, the list is the path: its boxes tick with Space. The colour follows the state (up to date, cycle out of date or missing, running, not in the coverage: the legend under the map names them in words) but is never alone: a ticked zone has a thick outline and a fuller tint. During an operation, the map is frozen like the list. An outline that is only the zone's frame is dashed; the legend recalls it. When the map cannot come (offline without a map background in memory, no WebGL, or the frame does not give the outlines), the list stands alone, with no error message. When the map is zoomed in enough (the States map), the aerodromes whose procedures are already here appear as dots: green when up to date, orange when of a past cycle; a tap on a dot says its code and its cycle, without ticking the State. Farther out, only the count on the State’s line stays. It is a reading of this device’s base: nothing is downloaded or written.

<a id="data-route"></a>**Route (RAD, FRA points).** In the Procedures group, one more row: the EUROCONTROL workbooks of the route calculation. It says the cycles present on this workstation (“RAD of … · FRA points of … · imported n days ago”) and takes the pill of the other sets: “Not in the repository” with no import, “Cycle out of date” when the latest RAD precedes the cycle in force, “Up to date” otherwise. “Import from Downloads” first looks, writing nothing, for the `RAD_<cycle>_v….xlsx` and `eurocontrol-airac-<cycle>-fra-points-….xlsx` workbooks in your Downloads folder, then asks a question that names them (kind, cycle, size) and recalls the licence: EUROCONTROL Network Manager documents, use by the company, published in its private repository only. After “Import”, only the workbooks shown are compiled into `data/route/` (a cycle replaces the same cycle, the others stay) and a commit is made; the row then says the cycles present. With no workbook in Downloads it says so and asks nothing.

<a id="data-pickup"></a>**Take the downloaded file.** Some sources do not download by themselves (the SIA for France, NATS for the United Kingdom): you download them in your browser, then "Take the downloaded file" (the second button of the base panel, and under the "to be dropped in by hand" line of a download) looks in this workstation's Downloads folder for the most recent zip with the expected name (for example EG_*_DS_*_XML.zip). The plan first and one question, in titled groups, one line per file: **Will be taken (n)** (country · file · size), **Ignored (n)** (country · file: the reason, for example openAIP’s “hot” layer), **To fix (n)** (country · source: what to do, for example “rename the file without space or accent” or “drop EG_*_DS_*_XML.zip into Downloads”). Seven lines show per group, the rest scrolls. The files go into this workstation’s raw sources (data/sia/, data/sia/zones/gb/…), never committed. Nothing found: no question, the screen says what is missing. The plan also sends the “personal” box: if the NATS sets are already there but the box does not declare NATS, the screen says “n NATS set(s) already taken: nothing new to take” (not “Nothing to take”) then “n NATS sets in place: the “personal” box does not declare NATS”: tick the box, then “Update” reads them. At the end: "Taken: n files (x MB)", the failures named (zip too large, nothing useful in the zip…) and "Now start Build". "Update all" never starts this take: it stays a gesture. The button is greyed with its reason if this workstation's launcher does not know the operation yet. The take only brings the zip the plan showed (name and size): if another zip arrived during the question, that source is refused ("the file changed since the plan") and nothing is taken for it; "Run the plan again" shows it anew. A zip whose name is outside the rule (letters, digits, dot, hyphen, underscore, space, parentheses) is shown by the plan but never taken: its line says "file name to rename: letters, digits, . _ -"; if nothing else is left to take, no question is asked. The openAIP exports (GeoJSON or JSON files dropped in Downloads) are not a zip: the plan shows the files one by one ("n files (x KB), of which n converted from JSON to GeoJSON") and the take sends back exactly what the plan showed. An export the suite does not read is said, never taken: an XML ("is not read (XML format): export it as GeoJSON or JSON on openaip.net") or the "hot" layer ("is not used"). If that is all there is, no question is asked.

<a id="data-purge"></a>**Purge.** The "Purge" button removes from this workstation the raw sources of the ticked zones (the ones you download again) and their caches. It first works out the plan without erasing anything, then asks one question: "Purge 15 files (14.3 MB) from the sources of Spain, France?". If the SIA files (France) are part of it, the question recalls that they also serve the outlines of the other zones when building; files tracked by git are always kept and counted. No: nothing is removed. Yes: the purge runs and the screen says "Purged: n files, x MB", with the paths refused (left in place). Nothing to remove: "Nothing to purge", with no question.

<a id="data-publish"></a>**Publish the base.** Under "Build", the "Publish the base" panel sends what was built on this workstation to the data repository. A built zone is committed whole on the workstation and also leaves at the next synchronisation: “Publish” sends it right away. It first says where the workstation stands: "3 files changed · 2 commits to send", the date of the last pass and, if there is one, the merge waiting in data/ (publishing concludes it first). Tap "Publish": one question recalls the files and the commits, then the pass runs; an endless bar shows that it is working. Meanwhile the buttons of the "Download, build, purge" panel are grey, and "Publish" is grey during any operation of that panel: one operation at a time. If the publication fails, the screen says why in the pilot's words and at which step (“The publication failed: the server refused the upload: a repository rule, or a file too big (step push)”); nothing is lost, the files stay on the workstation. Causes: the server's refusal, the remote repository changed meanwhile, Git LFS, login refused, server unreachable, conflict, busy repository, time-out, launcher error; with an older launcher the screen says so instead of guessing.

<a id="data-publish-end"></a>**At the end of the publication.** A notification gives the counts and the result stays in the panel: what was sent (files committed, files received); the files of the base kept on the workstation because a build was under way; the conflicts (the most recent is kept, see Settings › Conflicts); the files refused because they are not in the format of a data file. A failure says so in one sentence, with what the launcher answers: nothing is lost, the files stay on the workstation. The button is grey with its reason written below: "The workstation launcher is needed", "A build is running: publish when it is finished", "Nothing to publish", "The frame cannot publish the base yet: update the app", "Offline".

<a id="data-set-panels"></a>**The same buttons for every group.** Each group has its "Download · Build · Purge" panel: the Aeronautical base, the Procedures, the VFR points of the VAC charts (in the Aeronautical base group, under "Publish") and the Aircraft. The principle is the same everywhere: one operation at a time, all panels together (it is found again when you reopen the screen); one line per item while it runs, an end sentence and a notification, a line in the journal; "Purge" first works out a plan without deleting anything, then asks one question. Procedures: tick the States that are read in bulk, France (the SIA's eAIP) and the United States (the FAA's CIFP); none is ticked for you. States read through EAD Basic are never read in bulk: one aerodrome at a time, with "Read again" in Fuel or on the Map. VFR points: the cycle in force, no box to tick; "Download" fetches the cycle's VAC charts ("n charts not published yet" is not a failure); "Build" first needs this cycle's France base, the downloaded charts and pypdf (the first-install assistant installs it), and says when nothing was committed for lack of a trigram; "Purge" empties the charts cache of all cycles. Aircraft: ICAO types separated by commas or spaces (2 to 4 letters or digits); a refused code is said and nothing is sent, an empty field means the fleet's types; the lines of the run are the three steps (types, performance, SimBrief profiles). The weather has no panel: it is published with the site. A button greyed with "This workstation's launcher does not know this operation yet": the launcher of this workstation is older than the app, update it. Nothing is committed by "Download" or "Purge". "Build" commits its results on the workstation, except for the aircraft: this workstation's AircraftDB/ folder is not versioned, sending is done by "Published sheets". While a merge is waiting in data/, no build or sheets publication writes anything: the screen says "A merge is waiting in data/: synchronise first (Settings › Synchronisation)". During an update of the suite no operation starts (base, other sets, chain, file pickup, sheets): the screen says "Suite update under way: try again after the launcher restarts"; the state and the following of a job already running go on.

<a id="data-aircraft-sheets"></a>**The airline's published sheets.** Under the Aircraft, "Publish" copies the airline's sheets to the data repository. It first works out a plan without changing anything, then asks one question (sheets to copy, sheets to remove, total size); if you accept, the copy, the commit and the sending are done in one gesture. It needs your trigram (Settings › Identity); without it the screen says so and nothing is sent. With nothing to copy or remove: "Nothing to publish".

<a id="data-update-all"></a>**Update all.** The “Update all (n)” button at the top of the screen does the month's refresh in one gesture: the base (the sources to download, the zones to rebuild) then the other sets that have something to do (procedures, VFR points, aircraft), in the published order, with a single question (the plan: the AIRAC cycle, what will be touched, the size) and a single publication at the end. Beside it, a sentence says what it will do; “Base only (n)” limits it to the base. A failure does not stop the others: the summary lists each failure with its reason and “Try again”. A chain line shows the set and the step in progress, with a single “Stop”. Each other set is a **folded line** (“Procedures · up to date”) that, once unfolded, keeps its own “Update”; a country and a group keep theirs. An operation already running is shown by its card (which one, since when, how far, “View” · “Stop” · “Release”). **Shift + click** on countries of the map (or Shift + drag a rectangle; two fingers held for half a second on a phone) selects several: a banner offers “Add these countries (n)” or “Update (n)”. The journal keeps one line for the whole chain, with each set's result. The line of each other set **says what the launcher says of it**: the state verbs `proc/status`, `vfr/status` and `aircraft/status` are read once, read only, and their answer replaces what the screen used to deduce from the publication (“Procedures · to do — LF: downloaded, not built”, “VFR points · to do — incomplete: 28 of 454”, “Aircraft · up to date”); “up to date” means what the workstation **published** for the cycle in force, never what it merely downloaded. “Update all” relies on the same states. An older launcher without these verbs keeps the old deduction.

<a id="data-registry"></a>**The French aircraft register.** Under “Seen by the workstation, not read yet”, the “French aircraft register” fold is where the DGAC registrations file (`export.csv`) is deposited: its state (“deposited …” or “not deposited”), the link of the site to download it from, then “Choose the file…”. The workstation recognises it by its columns, keeps only five of them (registration, maker, model, serial number, home aerodrome), never an owner's name or address, and does not keep the original file; it says how many registrations it read. It is used to create an aircraft (“Deposit it in Data” leads here: `#data?registry=1`).

<a id="data-coverage-table"></a>**Coverage by country.** Under the groups, a folded “Coverage by country” block (nothing is read until you open it) asks the launcher what the base holds for each country: one row per country, seven columns — the country, **Points, Airways, Airspaces, FIR, Procedures, Ground** — each a number or “no” (none). Touch a heading to sort (the same one reverses), a country to see its cycle, its **sources read with their licence** and those not read yet. If the launcher is busy with another job, “Read again” asks again when it is over.

<a id="data-steps"></a>**A file to drop in by hand, step by step.** On the line of a zone that waits for a file only you can drop in, under the “Take the downloaded file” button, the list of steps: the **direct link** of the page where the file is (and, if that link no longer answers, the site’s home page), **the exact name** of each file to download with what it brings, then the button of the line. **A set with mixed cycles** (the VFR points, for example, partly of the current cycle and partly of an older one) never says “up to date”: its line says “partly up to date: 28 in 2611, 385 in 2610” (and, if any, “n of unknown cycle”). Beside that button, **“Choose the file…”** opens the system chooser: the chosen file is sent to the workstation’s launcher (a progress bar, “Cancel”), which **recognises it by its content** (SIA, NATS, LFV, openAIP) and puts it where “Take the downloaded file” reads; the pickup (the plan, the question, the build of the zone) follows by itself. A file that is not recognised, empty, too large (1.5 GB at most) or of another type is refused, with the reason in words.

<a id="data-inventory"></a>**The data inventory.** Under the groups, a folded « Data inventory » block says what “data” covers on this workstation: seven lines (the data repository, the folder local to the workstation, the raw SIA sources, this device’s copy, the suite’s working folders, Downloads, the backups left behind), each with its size, “tracked”, “local” or “outside the repository”, and who writes it. It is read only when you open it. It reads no file, moves nothing, writes nothing; without a launcher, or with an old one, it says “inventory unreadable”. With a launcher that can do it, it also offers “Put the raw sources away”: the raw SIA XML files of data/sia/ go into data/local/sources/sia/<cycle>/. The plan comes first (the files and their size, never a path) and nothing moves before you confirm; each file is copied, checked, then the old one removed (kept if git tracks it, never deleted for every workstation). The result says which files were moved, already in place, kept and refused with their reason; “Put them back” undoes the move the same way. Nothing goes while another operation runs or a merge is pending, nor from a paired device.

<a id="data-journal"></a>**The journal.** At the foot of the screen, the last 50 operations of this device: UTC time, verb, zones, result. These three blocks (coverage by country, data inventory, journal) are gathered in **one closed fold “Base details”** at the foot of the screen: open it to read, nothing is done there.

## Special cases

- **Without a launcher**: the screen reads the same way; it shows what is published.
- **A read that fails**: the section says so in one line, the rest of the screen is drawn.

# Aircraft

The company's fleet, as the other screens read it: the report (registration → type, callsigns), the map, the CPCO. The performance sheets are in the same screen ("Sheets" tab); the type registry will come.

## How to use it

<a id="aircraft-fleet"></a>**The fleet.** One row per aircraft: registration, type, callsigns, base, "out of service" when it is. The search filters on the registration, the type, a callsign or the base; seven rows are shown, "N more" unfolds the rest. "Add" opens an empty aircraft. The fleet is the one of the airline chosen in Settings › Airline; changing the airline changes the list, and an aircraft added goes into that airline's fleet. When the search finds nothing, "Create F-BHHH" opens a new aircraft with the typed registration, and "Search on rzjets.net" opens that site in a new tab at this registration (the suite never reads it: come back to enter the type). **The French register.** When the French aircraft register is deposited on this workstation (Data screen), a registration it knows is read under "No aircraft matches": "F-BHHH: CESSNA 172 S (French register, data of …)", the home aerodrome when the register carries it, and the proposed type — a suggestion from the register's label, to check; a type the type database does not recognise is said as it is, never guessed. "Create as registered" opens the filled sheet. In a new aircraft, "Search the French register" lists the registrations of a type (seven, then "n more"); choosing one fills the sheet. The register holds no person's name; nothing is saved before "Save". Without the register, a line says so, with the link to Data.

<a id="aircraft-edit"></a>**Editing an aircraft.** A tap on a row opens it: identity (registration, type, variant, owner or operator: an airline, a flying club, a private owner) and operation (callsigns separated by commas, base, tactical number, in service). Under the fields, a line says what will change ("2 fields changed: base, callsigns"); "Save" writes it. From two characters (upper case or not), the Type field suggests the types of the database and the matching sheets ("H145 · Airbus H145 · sheet H145"); a tap, or ↓ then Enter, writes the type code. A type the database does not know is kept as typed, and the screen says so. The registration is the key: two aircraft cannot share it. The location the site gives stays as it is. "Retire" asks, then the aircraft leaves the fleet; its performance sheet, if it has one, is kept.

<a id="aircraft-sheets"></a>**The sheets.** The "Sheets" tab lists the performance sheets by family (helicopters, turboprops, jets, pistons, others); the search filters on the name, the type or the registration. Each row says where the sheet comes from, what it lacks and its date; a fleet type without a sheet is offered "to complete". "Import a file" reads a CSV, a JSON, a .lnmperf or a SimBrief OFP; "Paste a text" reads a pasted text, a ChatGPT answer included; "New sheet" asks for the type code. Nothing is written at once: an import first shows, field by field, what would change; untick what you keep, then "Apply the selection". Several sheets at once: each preview names its sheet and its rank ("3 / 13"), a sheet that does not change is passed by itself, "Skip this sheet" goes to the next one without writing anything, and the end gives the summary; a note already written is replaced only if you tick it. The chosen tab is kept on the device, and the link `#aircraft?sheet=<id>` opens a sheet's editor directly.

<a id="aircraft-sheet-edit"></a>**Editing a sheet.** A tap on its name (or its line) opens the editor: the fields by group (identity, fuel, runway and alternate, climb, cruise, descent, RPM), the RPM / fuel flow table on a large screen, and the EUROCONTROL completion, "Complete from another sheet…" (a button that opens the search of the sheets, nothing chosen in advance) or "Complete with ChatGPT", which copies the request and opens ChatGPT in a new tab: paste the request there if it is not, then paste its answer under the button. "Fuel as volume" and "Fuel density" are explained under the field; a density outside 0.5 to 1.0 is refused. An RPM / fuel-flow table whose flow goes down while the RPM goes up is flagged (the row in orange, "a range misread?"); nothing is corrected without you. A dot "•" at the end of a filled field's label gives its origin and date ("lnmperf · 28/10", "edited · 30/10") on hover or a tap, with the discarded values; each card's title recalls its sources once ("Sources: V2 base · 28/10, edited · 30/10"). "Save" opens the preview of the changes, which are written only after "Apply the selection"; each changed field thus keeps its source, and the old value is kept. "Export .lnmperf" and "Export JSON" download the sheet; "Delete" asks before removing the sheet from the shared database. An altitude in the "ft" column makes a curve of its own: the rows of that altitude; if its flow goes down while the RPM goes up, a line says so with the altitude ("… at 8000 ft: a range misread?") and marks the row, correcting nothing. Under the table, "Propose from the measurements" reads one or more Fuel Phase Monitor journals (.csv, .csv.gz) chosen on the device (they are neither kept nor sent): the steady levels of the sheet's type are listed by altitude and RPM, with the deviation from the curve of the same altitude ("sheet 40, deviation -5 %") or "new row"; tick the ones to keep, "Add the ticked rows to the draft", then "Save" and "Apply" as for any change. With several altitudes, the table is one folding group per altitude ("8000 ft · 4 settings"); each row also has an optional TAS (kt), which Fuel reads when two rows frame the cruise RPM. On the workstation, "From the Output folder" lists the journals of X-Plane's Output folder (set once with "Set the Output folder"): tick them, "Read the ticked journals", and the levels are shown the same way. At the head of the table, "Curves" draws the fuel flow against the RPM, one colour per altitude with its legend; the measured levels are added as squares. The measured climb and descent each have a line there (RPM, fuel flow, rate, airspeed, throttle, and the sheet's current value): ticked, it fills the climb or the descent of the draft. The editor's top line stays visible: the sheet's name and its unit ("lb · lb/h"); a tap on the unit leads to its choice. The levels are put on significant levels (up to 5,000 ft by 1,000, then FL100, 150, 200 … 450: an FL181 counts as FL200); a level without measurement between two measured ones is computed, greyed and said "extrapolated". The measurements accepted stay on the device for a later computation, never published.

<a id="aircraft-fuel-volume"></a>**Fuel as volume.** Ticked: the quantities of this sheet (usable, taxi, reserve, flows) are volumes — litres or gallons, as the simulator shows them —, not masses; the density below converts them to kilograms.

<a id="aircraft-fuel-density"></a>**Density.** Kilograms per litre of the fuel: Jet A-1 about 0.80, Avgas about 0.72. It belongs to the fuel, not to the aircraft: leave it empty for the airline’s value (Settings), else the typical one. Between 0.5 and 1.0.

**Fuel units.** At the top of the editor, "Fuel read and typed in" offers kg, lb, l or US gal (and kg/h, lb/h, l/h, gal/h for the flows); each field and the RPM / fuel-flow table carry the unit chosen. A converted value is written with two significant digits (12,407.6 lb/h reads 12000); a value you typed stays as you wrote it, and the sheet keeps its value as long as you do not touch the field. The sheet keeps its stored unit: a line says it with the density used; a typed value is converted when saved, an untouched value does not change.

## Good to know

- The Site module also updates the fleet from the pasted "Fleet" page of the site: both write the same file, each save reads the file just before and changes one aircraft only.
- Nothing leaves before the next synchronisation.

# Settings

The settings of this device (⚙ at the top of the screen): your identity, what the app shows you, how data are
exchanged, and what the device keeps offline. They stay on the device, never synchronised.

## How to use

**The status.** The screen opens on the “Status” block, with the same dot as the ⚙ button of the header (green: something
new; amber: attention). One line per thing, touch it to act: the flight in progress (to the Map), the reports to write (to the
pre-filled report), the missing trigram (to “Identity”), the aeronautical database while it is not ready (to “Data
access”), the state of the data and the exchange in use (to “Synchronisation”, or the conflicts), the launcher, “Offline” and,
on a phone, the position during a flight.

**The groups.** At the Discovery level, the screen shows seven cards at most, one under the other: Identity, Data
access, Synchronisation (when it is used), Usages, Level, Design and About. The backup, the offline data, the weather
key, the Little Navmap sheets and the computer access come at the Everyday level. At the Everyday and Full levels the screen is ranged **by frequency of use**: always visible, the “Status” block, the display (usage level, design) and the device's airline; what is used now and then is folded, with its frequency on the right: Data (once a month), Synchronisation (when something is wrong), Device; Administration is a “›” row that opens its own page (with “← Settings” to come back); what is used only once is at the very bottom, folded, under “Getting started” (identity, access, create or join an airline); “About” closes the screen. Touch a folded title to open it. A group that needs attention (a warning,
an error, or the card an amber status line leads to) opens by itself, with an amber dot; a line of the “Status” block
opens the group of its card. A group opened by hand stays open while the page is open. The cards of circumstance (V2
settings found, configuration received, joining resume) come first, at every level, and go once dealt with. A card
opened by its address (the “Offline data” button of the “Join the airline” assistant, for instance) is shown alone,
whatever the level; “← Settings” goes back to the list.

**Your identity.** In “Identity”, type your trigram and your IVAO VID, VATSIM CID and SimBrief IDs, then “Save”. The
tools use them to prefill; with several users, the trigram is needed.

**The level.** “Level” chooses what the menu shows: Discovery, Everyday or Full, with one tap. Under each choice, the
“Menu:” line says which tools it shows; a hidden tool stays reachable by its address.

**The design.** “Design” shows one thumbnail per look — Standard, Cabinet, Rugged — and the tone: Automatic (as the
system), Day or Night. One tap applies it at once, on this device only. A design reserved for the computer does not
apply on a phone: the card says which one is shown instead, and its thumbnail is marked; touching a thumbnail replaces
that choice.

**The usages.** The sentence at the top of “Usages” says how data are exchanged. With no box ticked you are “Alone on
this device”: nothing goes elsewhere. Tick “Alone, on several devices” or “Several users (git data repository)” to
exchange. On a workstation served by the launcher, the app already reads and writes the launcher's `data/` folder, even
with no box ticked; an exchange usage also shares it through git.

**Data access.** On a phone or on the website, set in “Data access” the repository (account/repository), the branch and
your personal token, then “Test” and “Save”. On a workstation served by the launcher there is no token; while `data/` is
not a git repository, the card opens the door to fetch or take it over; its remote repository is set in the
“Administration” card (see “A git repository for data/”). On the launcher's computer, the “Data access” card starts with a “Data repository:” line giving the address and branch of the remote repository: the screen does not wait for it (the launcher may take several seconds to read it), the line shows “…” then its value, without moving; what depends on the repository (the “Remote repository” panel of Administration, for example) appears when it arrives.

**No access and no launcher, no bases.** A device with no access set and no launcher — the website opened on a phone
for the first time, for instance — has neither the aeronautical base (airspaces, airfields, beacons) nor the aircraft
base: “Offline data” says so. Two ways in: set the access to the data repository, even without ticking an exchange (it
then only reads the bases); or, on a workstation with the launcher, publish the bases to the repository (V2: Settings ›
Maintenance › Aeronautical data and Aircraft types), then synchronise. Three distinct states when the base is missing: “Aeronautical database loading…” (the read is not over: wait), “Data access not set on this device” (set the access above; an app added to the home screen has its own storage, nothing you set in Safari is there) and “Aeronautical database not published” (the access works, nothing is published in the repository).

**Synchronisation.** The “Synchronisation” card is the summary of what has not left yet: at the top, one count per kind (aircraft sheets, missions, flights, fleet; “Other” only when there is
something), then the last success, the network and the last error. A folded list per kind gives what is waiting: the readable title (for example “Sheet A320 Navy”, “Flight LFPO → LFBO of 3/11”), the date,
✕ for a deletion and, if there is one, the error that holds the queue; seven items visible at most, “… and n more” for the rest. With the workstation's launcher, “changed here” (not yet saved in the
data) and “to send” (saved, not yet sent) are added. “Send everything” sends and receives at once; otherwise synchronisation runs by itself at start, every 10 minutes online and when the network comes
back. The card appears as soon as an exchange is ticked or something is waiting. Below it, one line says what has just left (“Sent at 18:04: Aircraft sheets: 2 · Missions: 1”, with “of which 1 refused” if any); unfolded, it gives the files one by one by their readable title, the refusals first with the reason the transport gave, seven at most. It is the last synchronisation that sent something: a pass with nothing to send leaves it as it was, and nothing is said before the first send. On a workstation with a launcher, two lines say **how long the synchronisation took**: the last one and **the longest of today** (kept on this device), in seconds, step by step (settle, commit, receive, push…), with the number of git commands and what was sent (“8 s — commit 1 s · receive 2 s · push 4 s · 12 git commands (1 s) · 38 Ko sent”); a shorter pass does not replace the longest, another day clears it.

**The backup.** Alone on this device, “Export (one file)” keeps all your data in a file: it is the only copy. “Import
(merge)…” takes it back without losing anything here; “Replace from a file…” first erases what is on the device.

**Offline data.** Each published zone has its row: its state on this device, its cycle and its size. “Keep offline”
downloads it whole (preferably on Wi-Fi); “Free” gives the space back.

## The weather key

METAR works without a key. TAF, on a device without a launcher (phone, website), needs a personal and free avwx.rest
key: “Create a key on avwx.rest ↗” opens the provider's page. Paste it in “Weather key (TAF, avwx.rest)”, then “Save”;
“Test” asks for the TAF of an airfield, “Erase” removes it. The key stays on this device only (kept with your identity: it survives closing the browser), never in the repository

## Little Navmap sheets

On a workstation served by the launcher, the "Little Navmap sheets (.lnmperf)" card exports your performance sheets
to the .lnmperf files of a folder. The JSON sheet is the only source: choose the folder (Little Navmap's is offered
when found), "Save", then "Export the sheets" whenever you want; nothing happens in the background. A sheet without a

## The airline's API keys

On a workstation served by the launcher, at the Full level, the “Airline API keys (IVAO, EAD Basic)” button of the “Administration”
card unfolds the setting of the IVAO API key: it stays in the launcher’s `secrets/` folder, never in the browser nor in
the data. “Test” checks that IVAO answers; “Erase” removes it.

Under the IVAO key, the “EAD Basic (EUROCONTROL)” block takes the user name and password of your EAD Basic account: you give them once to this computer’s launcher (`secrets/` folder), which keeps them; the page never shows them again, it only says whether they are saved and when. They are used by “Read an aerodrome from EAD” (one aerodrome per gesture). EAD Basic is a demonstration tool with non-operational data, to be used for simulation only (the warning stays displayed). “Erase” removes them from the launcher.

<a id="settings-ivao-app"></a>Under the EAD Basic block, a link leads to the “IVAO application (pilots’ sign-in)” assistant (the card of the same name, “Administration” group, or `#settings?card=ivaoapp`). It lets pilots sign in to IVAO through the airline’s relay or this workstation: it guides you through IVAO (`https://developers.ivao.aero/create`: Name = the airline’s name, App Type = Third Party, Active = Enabled), lists the return addresses to declare with “+ Add Redirect URL” (this workstation’s, and the relay’s when its public address is set; “Copy” gives them exactly as they must be pasted), then takes the Client ID and the Client Secret that IVAO shows. The launcher writes them to `secrets/ivao_oauth.json`: you edit no file, and the page never shows them again (it only says whether the application is saved, and when). “Test” reaches IVAO’s OpenID discovery document; “Erase” removes the application. The application’s API key (`secrets/ivao.json`) is another setting: a new application’s key is carried over into “IVAO key”.

## The tablet next to the simulator

You fly on the computer (X-Plane) and want the map on a tablet or a phone: on the computer served by the launcher,
“Computer access” opens the Pilot application to devices of the same local network (or start `lancer.cmd --lan`).

1. **Open to devices of the local network**: the card shows the address to open on the device, its QR code and a
   8-digit pairing code (6 until 30 October 2026). Windows may ask to allow Python: accept for private networks only.
2. On the tablet, scan the QR code with the camera (or type the address), then enter the code. It is asked only
   **once per device**.
3. The tablet then sees the computer's data, the simulator's position in the flight badge, and “Launcher on
   <computer>” at the top.

- **Trusted network only**: everything goes over http, unencrypted. At home, yes; on public or hotel wifi, never.
- Access closes with the launcher, or with “Close access”. A paired device stays paired; “Forget the devices” asks
  them all for a code again (a lost tablet, for instance).
- On the tablet, the application does not work offline and does not use its GPS: the browser grants them only over
  https. The position comes from the computer's simulator.
- A code works only once and lasts 10 minutes: “New code” gives another. 5 wrong codes from one device: a
  one-minute wait; 30 in an hour: pairing closes until the next “New code”. “Forget”, next to a device (with its pairing
  date, in UTC), removes that one only.
- Only the computer's main address is opened; the others (VPN, virtual machines) only through “Open them too”. An
  address that cannot open is named in the card; the launcher's window says why.

## A git repository for data/

**GitHub or GitLab?** It is not a setting: the provider is read from the remote repository’s address and shown at the top of “Administration › Remote repository of data/”; the “Data access” card leads there.

On a workstation served by the launcher, at the Full level, the “Remote repository of data/” button of the
“Administration” card shows the remote repository of `data/`: its address and its branch. To set or change it, type the address (`git@host:account/repo.git` or `https://…`, with no password or token
in it): “Check” says whether the repository answers, “Save” sets it. Towards another address than the configured one, a Yes / No window opens on the computer, not in the page: the card says so before the click; answer it (it may be behind other windows), then come back. Refused, closed or already open: the card gives the reason and `data/` stays as it is. This is on purpose: a malicious page must not be able to send your data elsewhere. Nothing blocks: a repository that does not answer is
only reported.

- **Access to the repository** is set on the computer: an SSH key for a `git@…` address, credentials stored in Git for
  an `https://…` address.
- **The provider** of a configuration is chosen (github or gitlab), never guessed; the panel only reads the remote repository's address (GitHub, gitlab.com, framagit.org, or a self-hosted GitLab that the device's configuration names with the same host; otherwise “Provider not recognised”). At the top of the panel, “Provider: GitLab · project group/project” (the project is a link to its page); for GitLab, the “Pilots’ tokens” link leads to the creation of the welcome tokens; for GitHub, the panel suggests entering the address of a GitLab project, because welcoming by project token needs one. The page only suggests: changing the repository goes, as always, through the launcher's Yes / No window. The exported configuration always carries the provider (github or gitlab; an unknown repository is refused, never exported as “other”), and the pilot's side opens on it. An old configuration without a provider is accepted only for github.com. Without a launcher, a GitLab configuration is applied on the device like a GitHub one.
- **Several users through git** need this remote repository: without it, the “Several users” box leads nowhere, and
  the “Usages” card says so.
- **data/ missing or without git**: the “Data access” card opens a door, at every level. `data/` is a folder ignored by the code repository; it
  becomes its own git repository, linked to the data repository (not a submodule). Type the address of the data
  repository, and if needed a username and a password or token (never kept by the page; “Remember” hands them to Git on
  this computer). Without `data/`, “Fetch the data” clones it; with a `data/` without git (example data or archive),
  “Take data/ into this repository” links it to the repository. The card follows the work and reads the state again at
  the end; a failure is explained, nothing is lost.
- **Rewritten repository history** (for instance to purge big files from it): this computer's synchronisation then
  refuses any merge and says so; nothing is lost. Here, « Take up the new history » first shows what will leave (your
  commits, the data files carried over), asks, keeps the old state in a local reference, carries your data files onto
  the new history, then synchronises. A file outside the synchronised data is never sent again.

## Configuring a pilot

**Preparing the configuration (administrator).** In the “Administration” card (“Full” level), “Add a pilot” prepares a pilot’s configuration (see “Add a pilot (administrator)” below). It gives **three forms of the same configuration**: the file sog-config-<TRI>.json, the link and its QR code; send whichever you like. The pilot scans the QR code with their phone’s camera: it opens the link, which leads to the assistant. They contain the airline, the data repository, the trigram and the token how-to, never a token or a secret; the repository’s owner (user or organisation) is noted there, it decides which token to create. For a GitHub repository, first invite the pilot’s GitHub name on the repository, with the Write right (the link leads to the repository’s collaborators page).

**Joining the airline (pilot).** The QR code (scanned with the phone’s camera: it opens the link), the link, “Join the airline…” (“Data access” card: the assistant without a configuration asks you to paste the link received, or to choose the file) and the welcome card of the first opening open the “Join the airline” assistant, one screen per step: the
configuration received; the account (create it if needed, with the sign-up link) and the username; the invitation to
accept; the token, step by step (classic for a user's repository, fine-grained for an organisation's); the token pasted
and tested; the network IDs; and last “Finish and synchronise”, then the offline data. Ticked boxes stay on the device:
a settings card offers to resume where you left off. The “Join the airline” tutorial follows the same steps.

**Joining for a GitLab project (pilot).** When the configuration you received is a GitLab project, you create nothing: no account, no invitation, no token. The assistant has only three screens: the configuration read (airline, project), then “Paste the token” — paste the token the administrator sent you apart (never in the link); your trigram is read from the configuration, you do not type it — then “Finish and synchronise”. Your VID, CID and SimBrief identifiers are filled in later in Settings › Identity. Before accepting the token, the app asks GitLab what kind of token it is. A personal token (it works for all your projects) gives a question: prefer the project token asked from the administrator; if you answer “No”, nothing is saved. A token without an expiry date (or more than a year away), or with more rights than needed, is flagged with a warning line, without blocking you. The “Data access” card says the same of a personal token, without a question: alone with your own repository, a personal token is the right one.

**Nothing more than the file.** Only what the configuration holds is set, and the access is saved only once the token is
tested; “Test” says what is wrong: invitation not accepted yet, token copied wrongly or expired, token without the right
on the contents. A classic token opens all the repositories of the account: for a user's repository, a GitHub account
dedicated to flying is advised. A file in another format is refused with a message. The airline is written to the
repository only if it has none yet. On a workstation whose data/ already follows this repository, the assistant skips
the account and the token. Without a launcher, the app reaches a GitHub repository or a project on a GitLab instance
(gitlab.com): paste the project's address (https://gitlab.com/group/project) and the project access token the
administrator gave you; the token is sent to that instance only. When the received configuration or the device's access is a GitLab project, the “Data access” card expects the project's address and describes the project token (Developer role, `write_repository`).

## Good to know

- **IVAO account**: the « IVAO account » card links this device to your IVAO account through the airline's relay (or
  the workstation's launcher): « Sign in to IVAO » opens IVAO's page, then comes back here. The relay keeps the airline
  application's secret; your token stays on this device, renewed by itself, never in a backup. « Unlink » erases it and asks IVAO
  to withdraw the access; if IVAO cannot, the suite tells you to withdraw it in your IVAO account. Without an IVAO
  application set, the button is off and the card says where it is missing: on this workstation, Administration › « IVAO
  application » sets it (it gives the return address to declare at IVAO); on the relay, the administrator sets it.
- **Devices**: one screen, Maintenance › « Devices », for every pilot's device, whether it comes from the local network
  or the airline's relay (when the launcher runs as the relay, behind the NAS's reverse proxy, in https). A pilot pairs
  each device once with an 8-digit code the administrator asks for there for their trigram (once, 10 minutes, on either
  channel); their writes are signed with that trigram. Each row says the device's channel; a device paired with the
  console's code says « console code », and « Name » sets a trigram on it. A lost device, or all those of a pilot who
  leaves, are revoked there.
- **Update the suite** (Administration, with the launcher): choose a channel — stable, pre-releases, or development
  follow-up —, “Check” says what would change without touching anything, then “Update” (after a question). Never a step back;
  refused if files of the suite were changed on this computer, and while a job of the Data module or a synchronisation runs
  (try again once it is over). The data are not touched. The version taken is the one the suite’s repository announces: its
  tags are not signed, the trust is the same as an update made by hand with git. It is the one “Check” showed: if the
  channel changed meanwhile, nothing is written and the panel asks to check again.
  Then close the launcher’s window and run
  lancer.cmd again.
- **Folders of this computer** (Administration, with the launcher): where the suite, the data, the downloads, Little
  Navmap's databases and sheets are on this computer, the path read only and where it comes from; “Open” shows the folder
  in the file explorer. A path is changed where it says, never here; X-Plane is unknown to the launcher.
- **Pilots' tokens (GitLab)**: in the Maintenance application, on the launcher's computer, « Pilots' tokens » creates
  one project access token per pilot (named after the trigram, Developer role, one year at most) and revokes it. After
  « Create the token », the screen shows only that token, at the top: copy it at once (it will not be shown again), with
  what to tell the pilot and the link to their message (« Add a pilot »); the « Existing tokens » list is folded below,
  the new one there « created just now ». The launcher uses the administrator's token there, which it never shows: a personal access token of your
  GitLab account (Preferences › Access tokens), scope api, with an expiry (one year at most), this account being
  Maintainer or Owner of the project. Paste it in « Administrator's token », under the list; « Test the administrator's
  token » says the account, its role, its scopes and its expiry; the block's title says whether a token is set (its date, the
  instance, the project, the time of the last test), never its value. A refusal of GitLab is said by its cause: token refused
  (missing, expired or without api), account that is not Maintainer, project not found. The data branch must be protected,
  open to the Developer role and without force push (on gitlab.com the default branch is reserved to Maintainers): the screen checks it, and « Open the
  branch to Developers » does it after a question, asked too before the first token. A pilot whose token is refused
  writing reads « the project's branch main is not open to Developers ».
- **Token expiry (GitLab)**: the « Existing tokens » list says, for each token, « expires on … · in 12 days » (amber under 30 days, red under 7, « expired on … » once the date is past); the date is read from GitLab each time the screen opens, nothing of it is kept. On the launcher's computer, the « Status » block of Settings says « 2 pilot tokens expire soon (nearest: …) »: the number and the nearest date, read at most once a day, never a name. For its own token, the device keeps only the expiry date GitLab gives (never the token), reads it again at most once a day, says it in the « Status » block and, under 30 days, once a day in a notification. A refused token is said « expired on … » only when that known date is past: GitLab answers an expired, a revoked and an unknown token the same way. With GitHub, no date is read (the browser cannot read the header that carries it).
- **The token**: on github.com, a fine-grained token, on the data repository only, “Contents” read and write, 90 days
  at most; on GitLab, a project access token (one per pilot, named after the trigram), created by the administrator. It
  is never shown again; never paste it in a message.
- **Your settings stay after the browser is closed**: the identity, the token and the weather key are kept in the same place
  on the device, never synchronised nor in a backup. If Safari made you lose them anyway (it clears the storage of a site
  that is little used), add the app to the Home Screen: it is better kept there. The diagnostics (“Copy”) say whether the
  browser promised not to clear (`storage.persisted`).
- **Cut access**: erases the token from this device; your pending writes stay and will leave once access is set again.
  Lost or stolen phone: also delete the token at the host (github.com, gitlab.com…).
- **V2 settings**: if this browser was already used for V2, the “V2 settings found” card offers once to take over your
  identity and your access.
- **About**: links to this help and to the roadmap (what is delivered, in progress, planned), then the
  diagnostics, folded: transport, last sync, errors, repository tree, reference bases and aeronautical base, line by
  line; “Copy” puts it all on the clipboard, to paste in your message to report a problem. The token is
  never in it.

**The airline.** At the “Full” level (or through #settings?card=company), the “Airline” card lists the airlines of data/airline/index.json and “Personal”: the name, the code, the networks (IVAO, VATSIM, off-line) and the number of aircraft in the fleet. The card is titled “This device’s airline”: a list of radio buttons, one line per airline (the name, the code, the kind said in words — “Commercial”, “SO squadron” or “Personal” —, the networks and the fleet). Touching a line makes it the current airline of this device (the choice is per device): only the radio buttons change, the list is not redrawn. While the frame cannot yet save this choice, the buttons are grey and the card says so. Under the list, the sheet of the chosen airline (the others have none), open: the **kind** (two radio buttons, a sentence each) then the **mission families** (one box per family: the acronym and the name), ticked from the kind while nothing has been ticked; ticking or unticking writes themes.json, at least one family stays ticked. **Add a family** (an acronym of 2 to 8 letters or digits, a name of 1 to 40 characters; eight at most; a free **letter** is proposed to number the family's reports, refused with its reasons when a family of the table, M, D, X or another added family already has it; a family added before the letters gets a free letter proposed, written only when you set it) and **Add a series** (a family of the list, a name; twenty per family): the name typed in the language of the screen is copied into the four languages; the family or series is removed with “Remove”. Three gestures follow, in this order: “Create an airline” opens an **assistant on the same screen**: the **Identity** step (the name; the code, proposed from the name and editable, 2 to 8 lower-case letters or digits, never one already taken; the **kind**, Commercial or SO squadron, Commercial preselected; the networks), then the **Flight families** step (the kind's own, ticked in advance: untick what you do not fly; nothing is written for the families if you do not touch them), then the **Fleet** step (“Fleet · 2 aircraft”: search a type in the sheets base, type the registration, “Add the aircraft”; “Remove”; “Later” if you prefer to add the aircraft from Aircraft), then the **Summary** in words (“Rennes Motoculture · Commercial · IVAO · 1 family · 2 aircraft”). “Back” keeps everything typed. **Nothing is written before “Create the airline”**: then the settings, the (empty) fleet and, last, the entry in the list (an airline is listed only when complete); an existing file is never overwritten. If the creation is interrupted, the message says what is done and “Resume” finishes without overwriting anything. At the end, “Fly for this airline” chooses it and opens Missions. If the code typed already has its folder but the airline is missing from the list, the assistant says so and offers **“Take this airline back”**: it goes back into the list, nothing else is written. Twenty airlines at most; everything read from data/airline/ is treated as untrusted text. “Join an airline” opens another assistant: paste the “Fleet” page of the airline’s website (or “Read the clipboard”), then “Read the page”; the screen shows **what was read** (“4 aircraft · 3 operators”), the name and code proposed, the kind (“SO squadron” by default), the networks and the address of the site (optional, https) **before anything is written**; “Join” then writes the settings, the fleet read and, last, the entry in the list. The “what was read” step also says **how many missions of the registry are already at this code** (“Registry: 2 missions already at this code”), and follows the code you type. An airline already in the list is not joined again: its fleet is refreshed by the paste of the airline website screen. On the launcher's computer, while your airline is still at the root of data/airline/, the card offers to **migrate** it: an optional code (proposed from the name), “See what would be done” (the name, the folder data/airline/<code>/, the files and their size, the fleet that will be empty there), then “Migrate to “<code>””. Nothing is deleted or overwritten: the current files stay in place as a copy. In the sheet: with no kind every family is ticked in advance and the sheet asks you to choose the kind; a commercial airline has the Commercial family (cargo, passengers, empty leg, VIP, training, transit, test flights), a squadron the sixteen military families. “Personal” (every family) has nothing to set.
