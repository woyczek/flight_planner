- **Proposals of lot 1** (accepted):
  1. The studio and the generator write, per map, `profile_def` (copy of the profile), the resolved `bbox` and the
     `cycle`; the same goes for `vertical_profile.profile_def`. Older missions use the built-in profile of their type.
  2. Fuel writes the climb and descent figures with the flight (`profile: { climbFpm, climbKt, descentFpm, descentKt,
     tocNm, todNm }`), rather than maps computing them a second time.
  3. The frame allows the tile hosts (OpenFreeMap, VersaTiles) and the terrain tiles, and offers a "cache on first
     use" list so that MapLibre (1 MB) is not precached by every phone.
  4. Without tiles (offline, or refused), the map is drawn on a plain background with the country outlines of
     `aero_zones_geo.js`.
  5. Lots: 2 the route map of the planned flight, 3 the vertical profile with the common cursor, 4 the mission maps
     redrawn.
- **22 Oct 2026 — lot 2, the route map of the planned flight** ([SPEC.md](SPEC.md), [INTEGRATION.md](INTEGRATION.md)).
  - **Copies in `lib/`**: the frame's service worker keeps only `frame/` and `modules/`, so `aero_symbols.js` (with a
    header line naming its origin) and MapLibre are copied there. The country outlines are generated by
    `tools/build_maps_outlines.py` (350 KB).
  - **Base map**: OpenFreeMap `positron`, a light style under the ICAO symbology. MapLibre is loaded with a script
    element: it is a UMD bundle, not an ES module.
  - **Display profile**: a built-in "IFR route" profile in the module, derived from the studio's `ifr-simple` and
    `ifrRouteProfile`. Low or high airways and airspaces follow the flight level; aerodromes, navaids and MORA are on.
  - **Overview declutter**: below 120 px per degree, a whole route on a phone was unreadable (every aerodrome and
    VOR rose of France). Only what is within 15 NM of the route is drawn; closer views show everything.
  - **Route on top**: the route's labels are reserved first in the placer, and the route is drawn last. In the first
    version the symbols hid the point labels.
  - **Data area**:
    - overview: the route's area; closer: the view plus 15 %;
    - airspaces and airways: the route's corridor only. In the first version a portrait view of a west-east route
      opened eleven zones.
  - **Plain background**: static, fitted to the route; no pan or zoom without tiles in this lot.
  - **Test page**: `modules/maps/test/index.html` mounts the module outside the frame, which does not list it yet. It
    uses the real data layer (local transport, references from the launcher) and seeds a flight
    (`?flight=ifr|low|none`, `?tiles=0`).

- **23 Oct 2026 — lot 5: airway points, presets, density, tooltips, live view.**
  - **Reference**: Navigraph high-level IFR charts given by the user (boxed airway names along the segment, minimum
    level, segment distance, tracks, fixes as triangles, MORA "3⁵").
  - **Airway points**: the ends of the segments drawn. A navaid at the same place (within 0.05°) is drawn with its own
    symbol, even when the profile hides navaids; the other points are triangles. Their names pass before the track
    labels, which are placed last.
  - **Presets rather than a free profile editor**: four choices cover the captures (low, high, VFR) and the automatic
    one. Auto follows the level of the current flight (FL195, as the V2 `ifrRouteProfile`). The VFR preset takes the
    studio's `vfr-detail` profile on the topographic `ofm-liberty` base. The choice is per device, in the frame's
    cache (`cache/maps/ui.json`), never synchronised.
  - **Density by zoom** replaces part of the overview rule: three tiers by pixels per degree (80, 300). The thresholds
    were set from 375 px screenshots: the fixes appear from the mid tier, otherwise a phone showing France never sees
    them.
  - **Tooltips on the label's place**:
    - the airspace tooltip uses AIP notation, the same in the line under the profile (before: "FL 195 – FL 999");
    - airway tooltip asked by the user ("no rollover on airway names on the map"): `draw()` returns the boxes it
      placed, so the hit test uses exactly what is drawn, and needs no second layout;
    - touch: Chrome sends compatibility mouse events after a tap; they reopened the map's tooltip 450 ms after the tap
      that closed it, and the map's `mouseout` hid the profile's. Both are ignored now, and `mouseout` hides only the
      map's tooltip.
  - **`alt_ft` is a held level, not an altitude to reach**: fuel lot C defines it as the level from the point on
    (OFP "/F280"). It is passed to the profile as `lvl`, with `level.ft` on the first point; with `alt`, the profile
    would reach the level at the point and the steps would start one segment early. Missions keep the studio's
    meaning (altitude at the point).

- **23 Oct 2026 — lot 6, planned (design for validation, nothing coded): editing the route on the map and the
  profile.**
  - **Asked by the user**: drag a waypoint (snapped to nearby fixes, navaids and aerodromes); tap a fix or a navaid to
    insert it in the nearest segment; remove a point; drag a point's level on the profile (steps); each gesture writes
    the current flight, fuel recomputes, the profile and the quote follow; an explicit "Edit" mode on the phone; undo
    the last gesture. "Edit the route" towards fuel stays for typed routes.
  - **One rule on every device**: an "Edit" toggle button in the map box, top right (the preset list stays top left).
    Outside the mode nothing changes. In the mode:
    - a press within 22 px of a route point drags that point (MapLibre's pan is off for that gesture only); a press
      elsewhere pans the map as usual, so the map stays usable;
    - a candidate under the finger (fix, navaid, aerodrome within 20 px) is ringed; releasing there snaps the point to
      it (its id and position); releasing elsewhere puts the point back (no free coordinates in lot 6: fuel and the
      OFP form expect named points; free points can come later as `4530N00215E`);
    - a tap on a fix, navaid or aerodrome not on the route inserts it where it lengthens the route least;
    - a tap on a route point opens a small card: its id, its level ("FL280 from here", or "continues"), "Remove";
      departure and arrival are never moved nor removed here (changing them stays in fuel);
    - on the profile, dragging the level line of a segment sets the held level from its first point (`alt_ft`, in
      steps of FL10; on the first segment, `level.ft`); a level equal to the previous one removes `alt_ft`;
    - "Undo" (in the mode) takes back the last gesture, up to 20, in memory only.
  - **Writing** (fuel INTEGRATION § 3, merge rule `record`): maps reads the record, changes `route.points` (id, lat,
    lon, `alt_ft`), rewrites `route.text` in the OFP form (`ID lat lon[/Fnnn]`, one entry per line) as fuel does,
    `distanceNm` and `trackDeg`, sets `by: 'maps'` and `updated_at`, keeps every other field, and writes it back 400 ms
    after the last gesture. `meta.writes` gains `pilot/` (document policy, same declaration as fuel).
  - **The quote**: fuel requotes when it sees a record written by another module (`by` ≠ `fuel`), at once when it is
    mounted (the dual view shows both), else at its next mount; it then writes `profile` again. Until then maps draws
    its own TOC and TOD and ignores the stale `profile.tocNm` / `todNm`. Reviewed 28 Oct (lot 2): only `by: 'maps'` makes the profile stale — `by` is the
    last writer of the whole record, and the frame writes `by: 'frame'` with the live position (`progress.actual`), which
    hid fuel's profile. To agree with the fuel session (one line in
    its INTEGRATION § 3).

## The aerodrome procedures layer (28 Oct 2026, V3_SPECIFICATION § 10.22)

SPEC § 6.11, INTEGRATION § 3 ter. Choices of the session, with their reasons:
- **No event between modules** (decision of the ergonomics session, 28 Oct, replacing a first brief with `fuel-procedures` and
  `fuel-procedures-ask`): the map lights the layer by itself from what it already reads, the choice of procedures
  (`proceduresOf(flight)`) and the coverage of the two aerodromes. A contract between two modules would have been one more thing
  to keep in step; the rule is the map's, readable in one function (`covAuto`). "Nothing chosen" is `{}` and absent fields; `null` is
  "none", a choice, so it does not light.
- **The user's touch wins, for the page's life.** The stored state of the box stays "off" by default and the automatic state is laid
  over it, never written. Once the user touches the box, only the box decides until reload (not stored, not per mode), so the
  automatic lighting cannot fight a gesture. "Reset all" is not a touch: it restores the defaults, and the automatic lighting, if it
  was not switched off by a touch, stays on.
- **The box displays what is shown.** A box unticked while the layer is lit by itself would be a lie; ticked, it can be unticked, which
  is the touch above. The stored value is not changed by displaying it.
- **The pins are recoloured, not ringed.** The symbols already carry the aerodrome's class in their shape and fill (open to public
  traffic, restricted, State in red); a halo or ring would add a mark to every aerodrome and fight the labels. The colour replaces
  the blue (or the State red, while the layer is on: the level is the question then). Colours are the design's `--sog-ok` /
  `--sog-warn` / `--sog-err`, which the drawing context already keeps readable at night. The copy of `aero_symbols.js` diverges by
  two lines (`ad.cov`), noted where it diverges. Colour alone does not carry the meaning: the legend and the card say it in words.
- **Requests: visible aerodromes only, one batch per drawing, stale while refreshing.** `coverage` is cheap, but a world map has
  thousands of aerodromes in the data area; only those inside the frame (and drawn: public, or the close view) are asked. When
  aerodb says the coverage changed, the old answers stay on screen until the new ones arrive (no blinking, no flash of the usual
  colours), and a late batch from before the change is dropped.
- **The card line is always there** when `aerodb.coverage` exists, whether or not the layer is shown: the card is a detail view,
  and "why is this one orange" is asked there.
- **Not on mission maps** (their Layers content is the selector only), and not without a flight for the automatic lighting.
- **A new file, `coverage.js`**: `render.js` is already past the 60 KB of SPEC § 1; it only takes the drawing of the colour
  (`coverPins`, `cov` in `MapsRender.draw`); the requests, the rule, the legend rows and the card line are in `coverage.js`.
