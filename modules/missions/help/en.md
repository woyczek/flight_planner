# Missions

The airline's missions, in the Company application: those of the shared repository (written with the CPCO): choose one, or draw one at random, then prepare it in Fuel.

## How to use

<a id="missions-assigned"></a>**My assigned missions.** When the airline's centre assigns you a mission, it appears at the head of Missions, under "My assigned missions": the take-off (aerodrome, time Z), the aircraft, the callsign, and the crew's rendezvous and channels when the assignment carries them ("Open" for a link, "Copy" for a server or a frequency); "Prepare this flight" opens it in Fuel. The group exists only when an assignment carries your trigram; a cancelled assignment, or one whose take-off is more than 12 h past, is no longer there. Assignments are readable by every pilot of the airline: keep session passwords for the channel itself.

<a id="missions-list"></a>**The list.** The missions of the newest series first, grouped by family. Only the missions whose file is on this device are offered, in the list as in the draw; when there is none, the screen says "No readable mission on this device: synchronise". Each row gives the id, the title, the route, the aircraft type and the estimated block; "flown on …" marks a mission whose report you wrote. The search filters on the id, the title, an aerodrome (ICAO code), an aircraft type, a callsign (SAMU66) or the activity (MEDEVAC, HEMS, SAMU…), and also in the content of the missions already on the device: summary, description, objectives, risks, places, notes, callsigns and types, ignoring case, accents and hyphens ("clermont-ferrand" = "Clermont Ferrand"). When the word is not in the title, the row says where it was found ("in: risks"); a mission whose file is not on the device yet is searched on its registry line, and the list says so ("n missions not read"). Nothing is written or downloaded to search. The registry search is the same; the drop-down list keeps one family, given by its letter (the one of the identifiers), its short name and its name ("T · AIRLIFT · Airlift / logistics"), in letter order; a chosen family with several subtypes opens a second one, "Subtype", beside it. Seven rows are shown, "N more" unfolds the rest. The organisation missions of an exercise (those ending in Z) are not flights and are not listed. Only the missions of the airline chosen in Settings › Airline are listed; a mission that names no airline is personal: it is under « Personnel ». The list « My airline · Personal · All airlines », above the search, also shows the personal missions or those of other airlines, each with the name of its airline; they can be prepared the same way. When the airline has categories (cargo, passengers…), a second drop-down keeps one category, and the open mission says its own.

<a id="missions-open"></a>**Choose, or draw at random.** A tap on a row opens the mission sheet: its category, its summary in full under the title "Summary" (beyond 600 signs, "Read more" opens the detail), its risks (the first three, "hazard → mitigation", the others folded, or "No risk declared"), the teams engaged and its zones. "Detail" shows the rest in foldable sections: description (the detailed description only, without the summary), objectives, zones to activate, rendezvous, route provided, maps provided, payload and aircraft; "See on the Map" opens the Map on a zone or on a map of the mission (drawn from its coordinates). On a phone, the browser's back returns from the detail to the sheet, then to the list; on a large screen, the sheet and the detail are side by side. "Draw at random" picks one among the missions of the filtered list you have not flown yet; "Draw again" picks another. Nothing starts by itself: "Prepare this flight" opens Fuel, in the Pilot application, with the departure, the arrival, the points of the mission (as coordinates), the level and the aircraft type; the runway, the procedures, the fuel and the weather are then prepared as for any flight.

<a id="missions-drawers"></a>**The drawers.** A mission may carry a sequel you do not know at departure: its drawers (twelve at most), called “Drawer 1, 2, 3…”. On the sheet of YOUR mission — it is assigned to you, or it is the mission of your flight — the “Drawers” section takes the place of the zones line: a closed drawer shows its title and its key in words (“opens at 20:20Z (estimated time)”, “opens when the aircraft enters the zone shown on the Map”, “to be opened by hand”); its message and its briefing appear only once it is open, with how and when (“opened on time, at …”). A drawer opens by itself at its time while the sheet is on screen, and a notification says so. “Open the drawer” asks for a second tap within five seconds, then writes the opening in your mission record, which follows you on your devices. A crew mate's finding (zone entered, object found) opens the drawer for the whole crew — within the minute while your sheet is on screen, and the line says “received here at …” when it came later; their “Open the drawer” gesture opens it for them only, and the line says so. The drawers open in order: only the first drawer still closed can open, and it alone carries “Open the drawer”; the next ones say “after drawer n”. When your device does not follow a passage yet, or has no live position, the line gives the time at which the drawer will open anyway (“the drawer will open at 19:20Z”). Beyond seven drawers, the next ones are folded. At the Full level, “Reseal” closes a drawer you opened: for you only, and the drawers after it close with it. On the sheet of a mission you are not playing, only the line “Drawers: n” appears.

<a id="missions-add"></a>**Add missions.** « Add missions », next to « Draw at random », reads one or more YAML mission files (a file may hold several, separated by « --- » or in a `missions:` list), or a pasted YAML. Before writing, the screen shows what it read (id, title, family, series, airline) and what it refuses, with the reason (id outside the rule, exercise organisation, duplicate, YAML unreadable at a given line). « Add (n) » writes each mission at its place, `missions/<letter>_<FAMILY>/<ID>/mission<ID>.yaml`; a mission that already exists is replaced only when « replace? » is ticked. A mission without an airline is personal. They leave at the next synchronisation.

<a id="missions-airline"></a>**Change airline.** On a mission’s sheet, « Airline: … · Change » offers the airlines and « Personal »; the sentence « … moves from … to … » is said before « Write ». In the list, « Assign to an airline… » takes the missions shown (scope, family, search), each ticked: the screen says how many change and which are unreadable (left as they are) before writing. When adding from YAML, « Add these missions to » chooses their airline. Only the `airline` field of the file changes.

<a id="missions-registry"></a>**The registry.** « Registry », next to « Draw at random » (or the address `#missions?view=registry`), shows one line per mission as the registry `missions/mission_registry.yaml` writes it, with the state of its file: there, missing, unreadable or path refused; and the repository missions the registry does not know. As in the V2 table, a mission holds on one line: the sign of its state (✓ file there, ! to check, + not in the registry, … reading, ? not read: the screen reads 500 files at most and says so in the count), the identifier, the title, the family and the series, the origin and the destination; a tap unfolds it: the state in words, the number, the operation, the path of the file, « Edit » and « Remove from the registry ». The lines to check come first, then the others by series and identifier; the family filter (and its subtype, as in the list) and the search are at the head. « Update the registry » first says what it would do (missions to add, lines without a readable file, duplicate identifiers, former identifiers), then « Write the registry » adds the repository missions, keeps one line per identifier and replaces a former identifier by the folder's; a line without a readable file stays (the file may not have arrived yet). An added mission takes its file's series, else the current series of its family (a number). In an identifier (« A-001A »), the final letter from A to Y is a flight linked to the mission without suffix, Z its organisation. « Edit » changes the title, series, number or operation of a line; « Remove from the registry » takes it out, the mission file stays in the repository. An unreadable registry is never rewritten; an absent one is created from the files.

<a id="missions-editor"></a>**The editor.** In the registry, « Create a mission » (at the head), « Edit » and « Duplicate » (on an unfolded line) open the editor, which has the shape of a workshop: the map of the mission (a sketch of the places for now), the unfolding (« At the departure », then the drawers of the mission, said by their rank with their key, and « ＋ Drawer » — touching a card opens its sheet in place of the departure's: what opens the drawer (the pilot's gesture alone, an hour, a passage zone or an object to find chosen among the Places, the arrival at the aerodrome), its fallback time when the key looks at the aircraft, « Show the zone to the pilot », its title — which the pilot reads on the closed drawer —, its message, its briefing, its onward route place by place, « What the pilot sees » — the drawer closed then open, in the very words of the pilot's screen —, and « Remove this drawer »; an arrival can require stops, added and removed one by one; twelve drawers at most; a key the workshop cannot edit yet is kept as it is) and the sheet of the departure, what the pilot reads before leaving, in six groups (Frame, Teams, Places, Story, Risks, Timeline and maps). Each group says its content in one line; touching it unfolds it, touching it again folds it, one is unfolded at a time. On a phone the map unfolds by its button and a mission being edited opens with its groups folded; on a large screen the map and the unfolding are on the left, the sheet on the right. At the foot, « Save », « To complete (n) » — which opens, just above, the named list of what is missing, never blocking — and « Cancel ». The sketch shows: departure and arrival in blue (for a liaison, their position comes from the aeronautical database), the target in red, the zones in dashed circles, the turning points and the rendezvous. The Frame group (family and subtype, series, identifier, title, theatre, departure, arrival) and the Teams group (one row per team: callsign, type, service, role; « + Team » adds one, the cross removes it) and the Places group (one row per place: departure and arrival for their position, target, zones, turning points, rendezvous, with their position in degrees and minutes; « Edit » opens its fields, latitude and longitude in decimal degrees; « + Zone », « + Turning point », « + Rendezvous », « + Target », « + Object to find » (an object the pilot does not see while the drawer whose key it is stays closed: its kind, its position, its detection range, « found only when the pilot declares it »; its identifier is given by the workshop; its row, like the row of a zone, says the drawer that cites it: « key of drawer 2 »); a polygon keeps its points, still moved in the V2; renaming a place renames it wherever the mission cites it (the route, a drawer, a map, the timeline, the profile); « Remove » on a place still cited first says who cites it, then « Remove anyway » removes it with its citations, and « To complete » then says the drawer left without its key (its trigger point, its passage zone, the point of its arrival) or the map left without its centre) and the Story group (the summary, with its count of signs: the sheet shows it whole up to 600; the description, one paragraph per blank line; the main objective; the secondary objectives, one per line; the load, its mass and its passengers) and the Risks group (one row « hazard → mitigation » per risk; « Edit » opens the hazard, the consequence and the mitigation; « + Risk » adds one; a removed risk leaves its translations too) are filled there; what is typed is kept from group to group until « Save » or « Cancel ». The last one, Timeline and maps, gives the mission's events (one row per event: the time from the planned take-off, « H+30mn », a place of the mission or none, what happens; « + Event ») then the declared maps; « Propose maps from the places » adds the missing ones (general, target, rendezvous, zones), whose images are made in the V2 map studio. The organisation of an exercise (Z mission) stays in the V2. A commercial mission (family « E · CAT », a link flight) has a reduced Frame: departure, arrival, airline, load (passengers, cargo…) and aircraft; the title is proposed by itself (« LFPO → LFRS ») as long as you have not changed it; teams, risks, timeline and theatre are not asked. The family proposes the next identifier and the current series; only a missing family and an identifier without its form (« T-086 ») or already taken prevent saving, the rest is listed under « To complete ». « Save » writes the mission file and its registry line on the device: they go with the next send, and the editor says so (« changed on this device — not sent yet »). Only what you change is written, the rest of the file is kept as it is; « Save » without any change writes nothing. A file that uses YAML aliases (« &… », « *… ») does not open in the editor, and a registry that uses them is said unreadable and never rewritten: write the repeated object out in full at each place (or edit the mission in the V2); a changed identifier writes a new file, the former one is never deleted. A mission created without an airline is personal.

## Good to know

- The module writes a mission only through its editor (« Create a mission », « Edit », « Duplicate »); the rest of the mission is still written in the CPCO.
- A mission whose file does not come says so: "not found" (the repository has not got it), "not on this device yet" (the synchronisation brings it, the mission appears when it arrives) or "unreadable" (with the reason). Only the registry is shown then.
- A mission that came with the synchronisation without the registry naming it is listed all the same, read from its file, at the next display; no button to press.
- A flight being prepared is replaced only after a question; a started flight never is (cancel the start, or finish the flight, first).
- The envelope, the page-by-page reader and the assignments will come later.

- A closed drawer is not a secret: its content is in the mission file, which every pilot can read. It is a game's seal. Without accounts, a pilot of the mission who writes a false finding in their record opens the drawer for the others: the screen always says by whom, when and how a drawer was opened.
